# Canada — Federal baseline (applies to Ontario/AGCO reviews; NOT the US BSA lane)

**Scope:** Use this file instead of `federal-baseline.md` whenever the review lane is a Canadian jurisdiction — today **Ontario** (`jurisdictions/on.md`) and **Alberta** (`jurisdictions/ab.md`). It replaces BSA/FinCEN + OFAC entirely; it does *not* replace PCI (same standard, see below). This file carries the federal AML/sanctions/payments obligations; the jurisdiction files carry the provincial standards. Never apply US CTR/SAR/OFAC logic to a Canadian code path — and never assume the two provinces are interchangeable, because they diverge on age, self-exclusion, prepaid rules, certification scope and privacy statute. `ab.md` has an "Ontario contrasts" section for exactly that reason.

## Reporting-entity structure (read this first)

- PCMLTFA s.5 is the exhaustive list of reporting entities (REs). The online-gaming class is **s.5(k.1)**: *"the government of a province that, in accordance with paragraph 207(1)(a) of the Criminal Code, conducts and manages a lottery scheme, other than bingo or the sale of lottery tickets, that is accessible to the public through the Internet or other digital network"*. Land-based casinos are s.5(k), (k.2), (k.3).
- Criminal Code **s.207(1)(a)** permits only the province to conduct and manage. In Ontario the conduct-and-manage entity is **iGaming Ontario** (continued under the iGaming Ontario Act, 2024, in force 12 May 2025). So on the statutory text **the PCMLTFA "casino" reporting entity is iGO/the Crown, not the registered operator** — a private operator is not named anywhere in s.5.
- This is consistent with AGCO Registrar's Standard **6.02 Req 2** (align operator AML internal controls with those of "the designated reporting entity under the PCMLTFA").
- Residual uncertainty, stated plainly: the AGCO Internet Gaming Notification Matrix item **I2C** speaks of STRs "filed with FINTRAC by all igaming operators and iGO", and item **S3** requires operators to submit their *own* FINTRAC audit results. No primary source located that makes an Ontario operator an s.5 RE in its own right. The likeliest reading (MEDIUM) is that iGO is the RE and operators file/produce as iGO's agents or under contractual delegation — but a second reading, that some operators are separately registered (e.g. as an MSB, or as an RE in another province), cannot be excluded. **Confirm with the client's compliance team which legal entity holds the FINTRAC RE ID.**
- **Alberta runs the same structure with different names.** The *iGaming Alberta Act* (Bill 48) makes the **Alberta iGaming Corporation (AiGC)** the conduct-and-manage entity and **AGLC** the regulator, so on the s.5(k.1) reading the reporting entity is AiGC rather than the operator — the same ambiguity as Ontario, with the same practical answer below. Alberta additionally routes AML and financial reporting through AiGC alongside FINTRAC (see `ab.md`).
- **the operator's own operating position resolves this in practice: the operator is the reporting entity and registers with FINTRAC directly.** The client's Canada Launch compliance page states plainly: *"As a casino operating in Canada, the operator Casino is designated as a Reporting Entity under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act"*, with FINTRAC Reporting Entity registration, an AML/ATF Compliance Program, a designated FINTRAC Compliance Officer, and automated transaction monitoring all treated as pre-launch obligations with **no grace period**. Filing runs through the **F2R** portal, and an **F2R Access Report (iGO-DS23)** must be submitted to iGO at onboarding to provision staff portal access — so the two regimes interlock rather than substitute.
  - Weight this correctly: it is the client's own compliance position, not a regulator determination, and the statutory tension with PCMLTFA s.5(k.1) (which names the province) is unresolved. But for a code review it settles the practical question — **build for the operator to file, not merely to produce**. Emitters, schemas and submission paths for LCTR, CDR, EFTR and STR all belong in scope.
- What this means for code, regardless of how the question resolves: the gateway must be able to **produce** report-grade records at PCMLTFR Schedule 1/2/3/4/6 field fidelity, retain them 5 years, and **export or transmit** them to iGO and/or FINTRAC. "We are not the reporting entity" is not a defence for missing data. Treat filing as configurable, data capture as mandatory.
- Note PCMLTFR **s.75**: a registered charity running a scheme ≤2 consecutive days under provincial/organisation supervision is deemed to be the supervising body — no analogue for commercial igaming operators.

## Criminal Code framework

- **s.207(1)(a)** province conducts and manages; **s.207(3)(a)** conducting/managing without authority — indictable max 2 yrs or summary; **s.207(3)(b)** mere participation — summary.
- **s.207(4)** defines "lottery scheme" by reference to s.206(1)(a)–(g). **s.207(4)(b)** was amended by the Safe and Regulated Sports Betting Act, S.C. 2021, c. 20, s. 2 to read "on any horse-race" — that is the single-event sport betting legalisation. **s.207(4)(c)** (computer/video device/slot machine/dice exclusion) binds only paras (1)(b)–(f), **not (1)(a)** — which is why a province may lawfully run an online scheme. **s.207(4.01)** defines *slot machine*.
- **s.207.1 is the international cruise ship exemption, not single-event betting** — a common mis-cite; do not repeat it.
- **s.197(1)** "common gaming house" includes a place where *a fee is charged for the privilege of playing* — captures any unlicensed rake-taking operator. **s.201** keeping a gaming/betting house; **s.202(1)(e)** book-making/betting business; **s.206(1)** lotteries and games of chance; **s.206(4)** buying a ticket is itself a summary offence. **s.204** carves out pari-mutuel horse racing.
- **s.462.31(1)** laundering proceeds of crime — mens rea includes *"being reckless as to whether"* (added S.C. 2019, c. 29, s. 103). Penalty **s.462.31(2)** max 10 yrs; **s.462.31(2.1)** criminal-organisation nexus max 14 yrs (2024, c. 17, s. 371). **s.462.31(2.3)** lets a court **infer** knowledge/recklessness where dealings are *"markedly unusual"* or inconsistent with lawful sector norms (2024, c. 17, s. 378) — this is the single most design-relevant provision: weak or undocumented transaction monitoring becomes evidence.
- **s.354(1)** possession of property obtained by crime (actual knowledge only, no recklessness limb); **s.355(a)/(b)** split at **$5,000**.
- **ss.83.02, 83.03, 83.04** terrorist financing offences, each indictable max **10 yrs**. **s.83.05** list of terrorist entities (Regulations Establishing a List of Entities, SOR/2002-284); **s.83.08** freeze/no-dealing; **s.83.1(1)** duty on *every person in Canada* to disclose without delay to the RCMP Commissioner or CSIS Director; **s.83.11(1)** monthly determination duty — **casinos and gaming are NOT among the nine listed classes**, which are banks, credit unions, insurers, trust/loan companies and provincially authorised securities dealers.

## FINTRAC reporting obligations

All thresholds are **CAD or equivalent in foreign/virtual currency** (PCMLTFR s.125). Electronic filing mandatory if technically capable (s.131(1)); report all known information within an item even though schedules are singular (s.131(3)).

- **LCTR — cash in ≥ $10,000** single transaction: PCMLTFR **s.70(1)(a)** + Schedule 1. Exempt if received from a financial entity or public body, or a person acting for such a client (**s.70(2)**). Deadline **15 days** after the day of receipt (**s.132(3)**). Parallel record: **s.72(1)** (incl. sale of chips/tokens/plaques, front cash deposit, safekeeping deposit, credit repayment, fiat bet, sale of casino cheques — s.72(2)).
- **LVCTR — virtual currency in ≥ $10,000** single transaction: **s.70(1)(d)** + Schedule 4. Deadline **5 working days** (**s.132(2)**). Record: **s.73**. (Ontario bans crypto deposits per note to Standard 5.69, so this should be an unreachable path in an ON-only build — verify it is actually unreachable, not merely unused.)
- **EFT out — international EFT ≥ $10,000** initiated at a person's/entity's request: **s.70(1)(b)** + Schedule 2. Deadline **5 working days** (**s.132(1)**). **Domestic EFTs are not reportable for casinos.**
- **EFT in — final receipt of an international EFT ≥ $10,000**: **s.70(1)(c)** + Schedule 3. Deadline **5 working days** (**s.132(1)**).
- **Casino Disbursement Report — disbursement ≥ $10,000** in a single transaction: **s.71** + Schedule 6. Deadline **15 days** (**s.132(3)**). Covered disbursements, verbatim heads: (a) redemption of chips, tokens or plaques; (b) front cash withdrawal; (c) safekeeping withdrawal; (d) advance on any form of credit incl. marker or counter cheque; (e) **payment on a bet, including a slot jackpot**; (f) payment to a client of funds received for credit to that or another client; (g) cashing a cheque or redeeming another negotiable instrument; (h) reimbursement of travel or entertainment expenses. For an igaming wallet, (e) and (f) are the live limbs — a large winnings payout or wallet withdrawal is reportable.
- **STR — no threshold, includes attempted transactions**: PCMLTFA **s.7** — reasonable grounds to suspect the transaction relates to a money laundering offence, a terrorist activity financing offence, **or a sanctions evasion offence** (the sanctions-evasion limb was added by 2024, c. 15, s. 280 — newer than most vendor rule libraries). Content: SOR/2001-317 **s.9(1)** + Schedule 1. Deadline: **"as soon as practicable after they have taken measures that enable them to establish that there are reasonable grounds to suspect"** (SOR/2001-317 **s.9(2)**) — no day count. Take reasonable measures to verify the subject's identity (PCMLTFR **s.85(1)**), unless doing so would tip them off (**s.85(2)**).
- **Terrorist Property / Listed Person or Entity Property Report**: PCMLTFA **s.7.1(1)** — triggered whenever disclosure is required under Criminal Code s.83.1, a United Nations Act order/regulation, a SEMA Part 1 order/regulation, or JVCFOA s.7(2). Content: SOR/2001-317 **s.10** + Schedule 2. Filing deadline **not verified** in the regulation text (FINTRAC guidance treats it as immediate).
- **24-hour rule (aggregation) — PCMLTFR ss.126–130.** Two or more transactions of the same type totalling **≥ $10,000** within **24 consecutive hours** are deemed a single transaction where the RE *knows* they share an aggregation type. s.126 cash, s.127 EFT initiation, s.128 EFT final receipt, s.129 virtual currency, **s.130 casino disbursements** (requested by / received by / requested or received on behalf of the same person or entity). Operating rules from FINTRAC guidance: the window is a **static** 24-hour window you define (e.g. 09:00 Mon – 08:59 Tue), normalised to one time zone; aggregation types are **conductor, beneficiary, on-behalf-of (third party)**; and **since 1 June 2021 individual transactions at or above $10,000 must be folded into the aggregate report** rather than filed separately (a standalone report is required only if the ≥$10,000 transaction is not aggregated with anything).

## Know-your-client

- **Prescribed methods for a person — PCMLTFR s.105**: (i) a government-issued identification document containing name and photograph (federal, provincial or foreign, not municipal); (ii) information from the person's **credit file, located in Canada and in existence for at least three years**; (iii) the **dual-process** method — information from two reliable, independent sources. Corporations: **s.109**. Other entities: **s.112**. Exact paragraph lettering within s.105 not verified.
- **Casino verification triggers — s.103(a)(i)–(vii)**: (i) opening an account; (ii) a person authorised to give instructions who is not an account holder; (iii) a person receiving a **s.71 reportable disbursement**; (iv) a transaction requiring a record under **s.74(2)(a)** (extension of credit **≥$3,000**) or **s.74(2)(f)** (receipt of funds **≥$3,000**); (v) foreign currency exchange **≥$3,000**; (vi) EFT initiation requiring a record under s.74(2)(c) (**≥$1,000**); (vii) beneficiary of an international EFT requiring a record under s.74(2)(e) (**≥$1,000**). Plus **s.84**: verify identity of anyone from whom cash or virtual currency ≥$10,000 is received.
- Timing: for s.103(a)(iii)–(vii) triggers, at the time of the transaction (MEDIUM — per FINTRAC guidance rather than a paragraph read in full).
- **Beneficial ownership — s.138**: for entity clients, obtain and confirm the accuracy of information on directors, on persons owning or controlling **25% or more**, and on trust beneficiaries and settlors; if it cannot be confirmed, treat the client as high risk and apply s.157 enhanced measures. **Schedule 7** is a report of discrepancies in beneficial ownership/control information (added by SOR/2024-267) (MEDIUM — trigger conditions not verified).
- **PEP/HIO determination — s.120.2**: (1) at account opening — foreign PEP, domestic PEP, head of an international organisation, prescribed family member, or close associate of a foreign PEP; (2) **periodically** for existing account holders; (3) at **$100,000** — (a) a request to initiate an international EFT ≥$100,000, (b) a beneficiary of a finally received international EFT ≥$100,000, (c) receipt of ≥$100,000 in cash or virtual currency; (4) on detecting a fact constituting reasonable grounds to suspect an account holder is such a person.
- **PEP consequences — s.122**: on a **foreign** PEP/family/close-associate determination under s.120.2(3) → take reasonable measures to establish **source of funds or virtual currency and source of wealth** and ensure **a member of senior management reviews the transaction** (s.122(1)). For a **domestic** PEP or head of an international organisation, the same measures apply **only if** the risk assessment under PCMLTFA s.9.6(2) indicates high ML/TF risk (s.122(5)). **Deadline: within 30 days after the day on which the transaction is conducted (s.122(9)).** Account-opening measures are in s.121 (MEDIUM — not read in full).
- **Third-party determination — s.134**; **ongoing monitoring of business relationships — s.123.1**.

## Compliance program & records

- PCMLTFA **s.9.6(1)** requires a program; **s.9.6(1.1)** requires it to be *"reasonably designed, risk-based and effective"* — an outcome standard, not a checklist.
- **PCMLTFR s.156(1)** mandated elements: (a) appointed compliance officer; (b) written policies and procedures, kept up to date and **approved by a senior officer**; (c) a **documented risk assessment** covering (i) clients, business relationships and correspondent relationships, (ii) products, services and **delivery channels**, (iii) geographic location of activities, (iv) affiliate risk, (v) any other relevant factor; (d) written ongoing training program for employees/agents; (e) documented training plan plus delivery; (f) documented plan for a review testing the program's effectiveness.
- **s.156(2)**: before carrying out a **new development or introducing a new technology** that may affect clients, business relationships, products, services, delivery channels or geography, assess and document the risk **first**. A new payment method, PSP, wallet feature or KYC vendor triggers this.
- **s.156(3)**: the effectiveness review must be **carried out and documented every two years** by an internal or external auditor (or by the person if they have no auditor). **s.156(4)**: an entity must report findings, policy/procedure updates in the period, and implementation status **in writing to a senior officer within 30 days** after the review is completed.
- **s.157** special measures where risk is high: enhanced identity verification; keeping client identification and s.138 information up to date at a risk-appropriate frequency; risk-appropriate frequency of ongoing monitoring.
- **Retention — PCMLTFR s.148: at least 5 years**; for records other than account records, 5 years from the day the record was created (**s.148(1)(c)**) (MEDIUM — paragraph-to-record-class mapping taken from FINTRAC guidance). Records must be kept so they can be provided to an authorised person **within 30 days** of a request (**s.149**) (MEDIUM).
- Interaction with the province: AGCO Standard **1.09**'s 3-year retention is a **floor**. Retention config must be `max(provincial, federal)` = 5 years, and 5 years must survive any PIPEDA-style deletion request.
- Administrative monetary penalties: PCMLTFA **s.73.1** and the AMP Regulations (SOR/2007-292). **Penalty amounts not verified** — do not quote figures.

## Sanctions screening

- **Regimes** (there is no single Canadian OFAC): **United Nations Act** regulations — RIUNRST **SOR/2001-360** (dealing prohibition **s.4**, duty to determine **s.7**, duty to disclose **s.8(1)**) and the Taliban/ISIL (Da'esh)/Al-Qaida regulations **SOR/99-444** (prohibition **s.2(a)**, property *"owned, held or controlled, directly or indirectly"*). **Special Economic Measures Act** ss.4(1)(b), 4(2), with the operative prohibition and duties in each country regulation (model: Russia, SOR/2014-58 — prohibition **s.3(a)**, duty to determine **s.6**, disclose **s.7(1)**). **JVCFOA (Magnitsky)** s.4 prohibition, s.6 duty to determine, **s.7(2)** duty to disclose. **Criminal Code** ss.83.05/83.08/83.1/83.11.
- **Casinos are NOT in the "duty to determine on a continuing basis" classes** in RIUNRST s.7, SOR/99-444 s.7, SEMA country regs s.6, JVCFOA s.6, or CC s.83.11(1). But the **duty to disclose binds every person in Canada** (RIUNRST s.8(1), JVCFOA s.7(2), CC s.83.1(1)), and PCMLTFA **s.7.1** converts any such disclosure obligation into a **Terrorist Property Report to FINTRAC**. Net effect: screening is not optional — the freeze-and-report path is live even without a statutory screening mandate.
- **Ownership/control deeming — SEMA s.2.1** (added 2023, c. 26, s. 253; identical text at JVCFOA **s.2.01**): property owned/held/controlled by an entity a listed person **controls** is deemed owned by that person. Control = **(a) ≥50%** of shares, ownership interests or voting rights; **or (b)** ability to change the composition or powers of the board; **or (c)** it is reasonable to conclude the person can, directly or indirectly and by any means, **direct the entity's activities**. UN Act regulations use "owned, held or controlled, directly or indirectly" with **no numeric test at all**.
- **Consolidated list**: Global Affairs Canada **Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List** (SEMA + JVCFOA only; expressly not a regulation). Machine-readable feed is **XML only** (`sema-lmes.xml`) — **no CSV or JSON**. **UN Act listings are not on that list**: you also need the UNSC consolidated list plus the schedules to SOR/2001-360 and SOR/99-444, and the Public Safety Canada CC s.83.05 list. **OSFI no longer publishes designated-person lists** (it now only receives monthly OSFI-525/OSFI-590 returns from federally regulated FIs, due by the 15th) — any code or runbook pointing at an OSFI list URL is stale.
- **How this differs from OFAC in ways that matter to code**: (1) **different lists, multiple sources, one non-JSON feed** — an OFAC SDN/CSL-only pipeline produces false clears; (2) **no aggregation rule** — OFAC aggregates ownership across multiple blocked persons, SEMA s.2.1(2)(a) speaks of a single "person", so a US-style rollup both misses and over-blocks; (3) limbs (b) and (c) are **judgement-based and cannot be computed** — they need a manual escalation path, not a percentage field; (4) **no Canadian "strength of match" or fuzzy-match guidance exists** (searched, not found — treat as absent guidance, not permission to loosen thresholds); (5) on a hit, the outputs are **freeze + disclose to RCMP Commissioner or CSIS Director + Terrorist Property Report to FINTRAC** — three recipients, versus one OFAC blocking report; (6) the licence analogue is a ministerial **permit** (SEMA s.4(4)–(6) / JVCFOA s.4(4)–(6)) or a **mistaken-identity certificate** (RIUNRST s.11, 45 working days) — there is no self-service unblocking.

## Privacy & data

- **Which privacy statute applies is province-specific, and neither province is the obvious answer.**
  - **Ontario:** PIPEDA applies in full — Ontario has no substantially-similar private-sector law and no general exemption order (only Quebec SOR/2003-374, Alberta SOR/2004-219 and BC SOR/2004-220 have one; Ontario's sole designation is health-sector PHIPA, SOR/2005-399). Layered on top, operator-held player data sits inside iGO's FIPPA-facing conduct-and-manage framework.
  - **Alberta:** Alberta *does* have a substantially-similar private-sector statute (**PIPA**), which displaces PIPEDA for intra-provincial private-sector activity — but for core iGaming data the governing regime is Alberta's **public-sector** *Protection of Privacy Act* (**POPA**) plus the *Access to Information Act* (ATIA), because operators act as agents of AiGC. **PIPA is residual, not the baseline, and PIPEDA is relevant mainly to cross-border/interprovincial flows.** Alberta also adds privacy impact assessments before go-live and before any new initiative involving personal information, a published personal-information-bank directory, and a ban on selling personal information. Details and section cites in `jurisdictions/ab.md`.
  - Consequence for code: a single "Canada privacy" module labelled PIPEDA or PIPA and reused across both provinces gets the notices, the consent basis and the access-request routing wrong for Alberta. Access/correction requests for AiGC-controlled data must route to AiGC.
- **Breach of security safeguards**: report to the OPC where the breach creates a **real risk of significant harm** (**s.10.1(1)**), **as soon as feasible after the organization determines that the breach has occurred** (**s.10.1(2)**); notify affected individuals as soon as feasible (**s.10.1(3), (6)**); notify other organisations that could reduce or mitigate harm (**s.10.2(1), (2)**); **keep a record of EVERY breach**, reportable or not (**s.10.3(1)**), producible to the Commissioner on request (**s.10.3(2)**). Significant harm is defined at **s.10.1(7)**; the RROSH factors (sensitivity, probability of misuse) at **s.10.1(8)**. There is **no fixed day count**.
- **Breach of Security Safeguards Regulations, SOR/2018-64**: OPC report must be in writing with the seven items in **s.2(1)**; individual notice content **s.3**; indirect notice mandatory in the **s.5(1)** circumstances; **breach records retained 24 months from the day the organization determines the breach occurred (s.6(1))**. Offence under PIPEDA **s.28**: up to **$100,000** on indictment for knowingly contravening s.10.1 or s.10.3(1).
- **Lawful basis for AML data**: PIPEDA **s.7(3)(c.2)** permits disclosure to FINTRAC without consent as required by PCMLTFA s.7; **s.7(3)(d.21)** covers private-to-private AML sharing under PCMLTFA s.11.01(1). Retention limits (Principle 4.5) still bind any use **beyond** the PCMLTFR mandate.
- **PCMLTFR Part 8 (s.158 onward)** governs disclosure, collection and use of personal information by REs (MEDIUM — text not read). FINTRAC's own retention: PCMLTFA **s.54(1)(d)** 10 years, **s.54(1)(e)** destroy identifying information at 15 years.
- **OPC Guidance for processing biometrics – for businesses (final, 11 August 2025)**: **delete copies of the identity document immediately once authentication is performed**; store biometric templates separately from other identifiers; a breach involving biometric data will generally meet the RROSH threshold. Directly binding on selfie/ID-match KYC flows.
- Quebec players in scope? Law 25 (P-39.1) adds: prompt notification to the CAI and each affected person on **risk of serious injury** (s.3.5), a register of **all** incidents (s.3.8), AMPs up to the greater of **$10,000,000 or 2% of worldwide turnover** (s.90.12), and a **60-day pre-notice to the CAI before bringing a biometric database into service**.
- No federal data-localization requirement; cross-border processing is permitted with accountability under PIPEDA Principle 4.1.3 (MEDIUM).

## Card networks & PCI

- **PCI DSS is contractual, not statutory, in Canada** — same obligation, same version, as the US lane. No Canadian statute mandates it. Reuse the US PCI findings verbatim; do not re-derive.
- **Rails differ**: Interac Debit and Interac e-Transfer dominate Canadian deposits/withdrawals. There is **no ACH/NACHA**; the equivalent batch rail is AFT over Payments Canada. Code that assumes ACH return codes, NACHA files, or US routing-number validation will not work and will silently fail Canadian reconciliation.
- **Code of Conduct for the Credit and Debit Card Industry in Canada** (FCAC-administered, revised version effective 30 October 2024) governs surcharging, disclosure and complaint handling for card acceptance (MEDIUM — applicability to a gaming merchant's own flows not verified).
- **Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23, s. 177) + SOR/2023-229 — Bank of Canada.** A payment service provider must be **registered before performing any retail payment activity (s.23)**; application under **s.29** ($2,500 fee). Registration prohibition in force **16 November 2024**; the substantive obligations (ss.17–22) in force **8 September 2025**. Obligations if in scope: **safeguarding of end-user funds (s.20)** with a written framework, a **daily per-end-user ledger** (Regs s.15(2)(b)(ii)), annual senior-officer and board approval (Regs s.15(5)), and an **independent review at least every three years** (Regs s.17(1)); **incident notice to the affected party and to the Bank "without delay"** on becoming aware of an incident with a **material impact (s.18(1))** — there is **no hour-count deadline, so any 24h/72h SLA in code is invented**; **annual report by 31 March** (s.21 + Regs s.18(1)); **significant-change notice at least 5 business days in advance** (Regs s.20(1)(a)). Likely exclusions for a gaming operator: **s.6(d) + Regs s.3** (activity *incidental* to another business — the player wallet exists to fund play), **s.8** (EFTs between affiliates only), **s.10** (agents of a registered PSP). There is **no gaming or lottery carve-out** in s.9 or Regs s.4. Erosion risks: player-to-player transfers, balances spendable outside the gaming service, or processing for third-party brands. RPAA AMPs run to **$10,000,000** for very serious violations (Regs s.48(1)).

## US-control mapping traps

The codebase under review was built US-first, so the likeliest failure mode is a US control being assumed to satisfy a Canadian obligation. These are the specific traps:

- **CTR (FinCEN 112, $10,000 cash) → LCTR only, and only on money IN.** (the operator has correctly *identified* the CDR as a required report type; as at May 2026 its schema was unconfirmed and automated threshold detection was not built. So expect the report to be named in documents and absent from code — check the emitter, not the doc.) PCMLTFR s.70(1)(a) covers cash received. The Canadian-specific report is the **Casino Disbursement Report on money OUT ≥ CAD$10,000 (s.71)**, which has **no US analogue at all**. A US-first codebase will have zero emitters for it. This is the highest-value gap to hunt.
- **USD-denominated constants.** Every Canadian threshold is CAD or equivalent (s.125). A hardcoded `10000` compared against a USD amount, or a shared `THRESHOLD_CTR` constant, is wrong in both directions. FX rate **and its source** must be recorded (s.74(2)(c)(iv)).
- **Aggregation window.** US CTR aggregation is per customer per **business day**; Canada is a **static 24-hour window** with **three** aggregation keys (conductor / beneficiary / on-behalf-of) and requires ≥$10,000 singles to be **folded into** the aggregate report (ss.126–130). A calendar-day `GROUP BY date` under-reports and mis-shapes reports.
- **SAR → STR is stricter, not looser.** No 30-day clock: "as soon as practicable" (SOR/2001-317 s.9(2)). **Attempted** transactions are reportable. Suspicion of **sanctions evasion** is now a trigger (PCMLTFA s.7(c)). A 30-day SAR SLA, a "continuing activity 90-day review" cycle, or a completed-transaction-only trigger are each a defect.
- **OFAC screening ≠ Canadian screening.** Different lists (GAC XML + UNSC + SOR schedules + CC s.83.05), no JSON feed, and OSFI list URLs are dead. An OFAC-only screening service is a false-clear generator.
- **OFAC 50 Percent Rule ≠ SEMA s.2.1.** Canada's 50% test is one of three disjunctive limbs and has **no aggregation across listed persons**. Numeric-only ownership rollup misses limbs (b)/(c); aggregating logic over-blocks relative to Canadian law. Limbs (b)/(c) need a human escalation queue, not a field.
- **Blocking report → three outputs.** OFAC's single 10-business-day blocking report becomes: freeze under the applicable instrument + disclose **without delay** to the RCMP Commissioner or CSIS Director + **Terrorist Property Report to FINTRAC** (PCMLTFA s.7.1). One "notify OFAC" hook is insufficient.
- **CIP (31 CFR 1020.220) SSN/TIN identity → invalid in Canada.** There is no TIN element in PCMLTFR s.105, and the SIN must not be used as an identifier. A US credit-bureau lookup also fails the "credit file **located in Canada**, in existence **≥3 years**" condition. Expect to add the dual-process method.
- **Title 31 casino program + annual independent testing → close but not sufficient.** Canada requires a documented **two-year** effectiveness review by an auditor (s.156(3)) **plus a written report to a senior officer within 30 days** (s.156(4)) **plus a pre-deployment risk assessment for new technology** (s.156(2)). Annual US audits over-satisfy frequency and under-satisfy the artefacts.
- **5-year BSA retention → matches (s.148), but** add the **30-day production window** (s.149) and make sure the Ontario 3-year Standard 1.09 config has not been used as the global default.
- **FinCEN MSB registration is not transferable.** Canadian analogues are FINTRAC MSB registration (only if acting as an MSB) and **Bank of Canada RPAA registration (s.23)** — separate regimes, separate identifiers.
- **Reg E / CFPB error resolution → no Canadian equivalent.** The nearest obligation is the RPAA s.18(1) incident notice, which is regulator- and end-user-facing with a "without delay" standard.
- **Crypto deposit paths.** Ontario bans crypto as a deposit method (note to Standard 5.69), but PCMLTFR ss.70(1)(d)/73 still bite on any receipt of virtual currency through any channel. Confirm the path is unreachable, not merely unadvertised.
