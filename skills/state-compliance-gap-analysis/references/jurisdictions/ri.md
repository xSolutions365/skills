# Rhode Island — Rhode Island Lottery (Division of Lottery) / Dept. of Business Regulation

**Code:** RI · **Products:** sportsbook (online + retail), iGaming (online casino) · **Key authority:** R.I. Gen. Laws §42-61.2 "Video Lottery Games, Table Games and Sports Wagering" (the Casino Act), esp. §§42-61.2-3.3 (sports wagering rulemaking), -3.4 (iGaming rulemaking), -14 (compulsive/problem gambling programs), -16 (general requirements for online sports wagering); implementing rules: **RI Lottery Rules and Regulations, Chapter 20** — Part III "Sports Wagering / Online Sports Wagering" (§§20.23–20.40) and Part IV "iGaming" (§§20.41–20.54), edition dated 2/20/2026. Regulator = RI Division of Lottery (RI Dept. of Revenue); RI Dept. of Business Regulation has adjacent commercial-licensing/gaming-and-athletics jurisdiction (230-RICR-30-30) but sports wagering/iGaming rulemaking sits with the Lottery Division under the Casino Act.

## Payments-relevant requirements

### Licensing & change management

- RI runs sports wagering and iGaming as a **state monopoly**, not a multi-operator licensing regime: the Division operates/controls Casino Gaming at the Twin River Lincoln and Tiverton facilities (Bally's-owned), with IGT running "Sportsbook RI" as the sole online/mobile platform vendor since September 2019 (HIGH — primary rules Ch. 20.1/20.3 general-operation language + secondary market reporting).
- Sports Wagering Machines and their software must be certified by an independent, third-party testing laboratory before use; no changes to a certified machine's assembly/operational functions without Division approval (Rule 20.30.D–F).
- The iGaming Platform and every Online Slot/Table Game offered on it must be tested and certified — on an ongoing basis, not just at launch — by a Division-approved independent third-party testing company (Rules 20.53.E, 20.45).
- General Casino Act change-control: a ≥5% change in ownership of a licensed Retailer requires ≥120 days' advance notice to the Division and prior approval (Rule 20.3.F) — not sports/iGaming-specific but applies to the licensed Facilities.
- **2025–2026 change (HIGH on the fact, from secondary reporting; exact statute/regulation citation not located — verify):** in May 2026 the RI Lottery ended its six-year single-operator online sportsbook market, selecting Bally's for a tentative five-year second-operator contract. Bally's go-live cannot be earlier than 11/26/2026, when IGT's existing exclusivity expires. This means a second, independently-integrated payments/KYC stack is coming online in RI within the current planning horizon.

### Deposits & permitted payment methods

- **Online Sports Wagering** (Rule 20.38.A): Online Gaming Account funding is limited to (1) debits from a Division-approved debit instrument, (2) ACH or another electronic-commerce mechanism transferring from an account verified as Player-controlled, or (3) any other Division-approved means. Deposit minimum $20, daily maximum $10,000 (same rule).
- **iGaming** (Rule 20.52.A): funding via (1) debit instrument or prepaid access instrument, (2) ACH/electronic-commerce transfer from a verified Player-controlled account, or (3) any other Division-approved means; specific min/max deposit amounts are set administratively by the Division rather than fixed in the rule text.
- Credit cards are **not** among the enumerated funding methods for either product (HIGH — confirmed by direct read of Rules 20.38.A and 20.52.A). Secondary/marketing sources describe RI's digital lottery payment stack as declining credit cards for responsible-gambling reasons (MEDIUM confidence — no express statutory or regulatory credit-card *ban* was located in Chapter 20; the omission functions as a de facto exclusion unless the Division separately approves credit cards under the residual "other means" catch-all). Flag for verification with RI Lottery/IGT (and, going forward, Bally's) compliance before assuming credit cards are categorically blocked at the code layer.
- Deposits may not be accepted into a suspended or closed account, and the operator may refuse any deposit "for what it deems to be a good and sufficient reason" (Rules 20.38.B–C, 20.52.B–C).

### Withdrawals & payout timelines

- **No numeric statutory/regulatory payout SLA was found** (contrast CO's 24-hour rule and MD's 7-day rule): a winning wager is credited to the account "as soon as reasonably practicable" after the event is declared official (OSB) or per the applicable game's rules (iGaming) (Rules 20.39.E, 20.53.G). Confidence HIGH that no fixed numeric deadline exists in RI Lottery Rules Ch. 20 as reviewed; LOW/verify on whether a deadline exists elsewhere in R.I. Gen. Laws §42-61.2 not captured here (direct access to webserver.rilegislature.gov was blocked during this research — see Sources note).
- Withdrawal rails mirror deposit rails: check, cashier's check, money order, or wire issued by the operator payable to the Player; credit back to the debit/prepaid instrument; ACH/electronic transfer; or another Division-approved means (Rules 20.38.D, 20.52.D).
- No peer-to-peer transfers: the operator may not allow a Player to electronically move funds from their Online Gaming/iGaming Account to another Player's account (Rules 20.36.B, 20.50.B).
- Retail (in-person) wagers or payouts over $10,000 trigger enhanced identity capture (name, address, SSN/passport, examined ID credential) — a state-level analog to a federal CTR — plus dedicated recordkeeping for any payout requiring IRS Form W-2G (Rules 20.27, 20.28). This is an in-person/retail overlay, not itself an online-payments rule, but it feeds the same identity-verification data model used online.
- Unredeemed winning tickets/wagers void and revert to the Lottery Fund one year after the event is settled (Rule 20.33.C) — relevant to unclaimed-funds handling, distinct from the "dormant account" treatment below.

### Player funds segregation / reserve

- **No CO/MD-style segregated reserve requirement** ("reserve ≥ outstanding liability") was found for Sports Wagering or iGaming Online Gaming Accounts in Chapter 20. This is a genuine structural difference: RI operates sports wagering/iGaming itself as a state monopoly (the Lottery Fund backs payouts), rather than licensing private operators whose balance sheets need a reserve backstop like CO/MD's commercial-license model (MEDIUM — confirm no such requirement has been layered on separately by DBR-side rulemaking or a bill not captured here; worth an explicit compliance-team confirmation given how unusual the absence is relative to other states).
- Accounts may not be overdrawn "unless caused by issues outside the control of" the Sports Wagering Service Provider (Rule 20.36.E) / "payment processing issues outside the control of" the iGaming vendor (Rule 20.50.E) — an overdraft-prevention control, not a funds-segregation rule.
- All net proceeds must be electronically transferred by the operator into a Division-designated account daily or weekly, at the Division's discretion (Rules 20.29.D, 20.48.D) — a settlement/sweep-cadence requirement, not a player-funds segregation rule per se.

### Responsible gambling

- Deposit limits, wager limits, time limits, a timeout feature, and self-exclusion must be offered by the platform and enforced immediately (or at a Player-specified future time) for both OSB (Rule 20.39.M) and iGaming (Rule 20.53.B); once a Player-set limit is active, only its expiration or Division approval may loosen it.
- The Facility-maintained excluded/prohibited-persons list is checked before an Online Gaming/iGaming Account may be created (Rules 20.37.A, 20.51.A). A self-excluded Player forfeits any winnings, after tax withholding, to the Division; up to $150,000/year of such forfeitures funds the RI Council on Problem Gambling (Rule 20.4.B; R.I. Gen. Laws §42-61.2-14).
- Facilities must fund compulsive/problem-gambling programs (≥$200,000/yr aggregate) and education/prevention programs (≥$50,000/yr aggregate) (Rule 20.4.C; §42-61.2-14).
- Online Gaming/iGaming Accounts with no login for 16 consecutive months are suspended; reactivation requires re-verification and may require a fresh government-issued photo ID (Rules 20.36.C, 20.50.C). 3+ years of inactivity makes the account "abandoned"/"dormant," handled under RI's unclaimed-property statute, R.I. Gen. Laws §33-21.1-1 et seq. (Rules 20.36.D, 20.50.D).

### KYC / age / geolocation

- **Age requirement diverges by product — do not reuse a single 21+ gate for RI.** Sports Wagering / Online Sports Wagering minimum age is **18** (Rule §20.23.A.11 "Player" definition; enforced at Rule 20.24.H, "prohibiting persons under eighteen (18) years of age from participating in sports wagering"). iGaming minimum age is **21** (Rule §20.41 definitions, "prohibited for players who have not reached twenty-one (21) years of age"; R.I. Gen. Laws §42-61.2-3.4). Confidence HIGH — confirmed by direct read of primary rule text for both products. This is the standout RI-specific quirk: almost every other state uses a uniform 21+ gate across OSB and iGaming, so an age-gate implementation that hard-codes "21 for all RI gaming products" would incorrectly block 18–20-year-old sports bettors (and, in the other direction, a single "18 for RI" flag would wrongly admit underage iGaming players).
- Registration requires name, date of birth, physical address, and at least the last 4 digits of SSN, verified before wagering is permitted; a KYC failure blocks wagering until manual document verification (government ID or passport) succeeds — except an OFAC watch-list hit, which may never be manually overridden (Rules 20.37.B–E, 20.51.B–E).
- One Online Gaming Account per Sportsbook for OSB; one iGaming Account total per Player (Rules 20.37.G, 20.51.G).
- Geolocation/geofencing is required at registration and at every login + wager attempt, confirming the Player is physically within Rhode Island (Rules 20.35.B–C [OSB "requirements and conditions"], 20.39.H [OSB platform requirement]; 20.42.B [iGaming general provisions], 20.53.J [iGaming platform requirement]). iGaming specifically permits out-of-state account access limited to deposit/withdrawal and viewing/changing account settings — wagering itself still requires an in-state geolocation re-check (Rule 20.42.B).

### AML overlays

- Operators must comply with all prescribed federal and state withholding/recording/reporting requirements "including, without limitation, currency transaction reports, anti-money laundering, suspicious activity reports, tax withholdings" and fund-transfer-to-authorities requirements on withheld winnings (Rules 20.29.E [OSB], 20.48.E [iGaming]) — this is the primary state-overlay AML clause, largely incorporating federal BSA obligations by reference rather than adding RI-specific numeric thresholds.
- Confidentiality rules explicitly carve out information-sharing with "financial institutions participating in a program established in accordance with Section 314(b) of the USA PATRIOT Act" (Rules 20.40.B.2 [OSB], 20.54.B.2 [iGaming]) alongside sharing with the Division and as otherwise required by law.
- The $10,000 in-person wager/payout identity-capture rule (Rules 20.27, 20.28) functions as a state-level CTR analog for retail cash-adjacent flows.
- Prize payouts requiring IRS Form W-2G are subject to a state child-support-arrears setoff against the Rhode Island Family Court's CSE system before payment to the patron (Rule 20.7).

### Records, reporting & data

- Online Gaming/iGaming Account records — registration info, ID-verification method, open/close timestamps, every login, all deposits/withdrawals/credits/debits, and the account number — must be recorded and retained for at least 5 years after account creation (Rules 20.36.F, 20.50.F).
- Personally identifiable information and login credentials must be encrypted in transit and at rest on the Sports Wagering/iGaming Platform (Rules 20.40.C, 20.54.C).
- The Division (or its designated agents) has audit rights over the operator's books and records, including tax returns and IRS withholding/reporting records (Rules 20.29.C, 20.48.C); operators must submit financial/operating/statistical data on the Director's schedule and format (Rules 20.29.B, 20.48.B).
- Net proceeds sweep to a Division-designated account daily or weekly at the Division's discretion (Rules 20.29.D, 20.48.D).

## Code review focus

- **Age gate must branch by product, not be a single RI constant:** 18+ for sports wagering, 21+ for iGaming (Rules §20.23.A.11 / Rule 20.24.H vs. §20.41 definitions). Search the codebase for any RI age constant and confirm it isn't shared across both product paths.
- No fixed payout SLA to enforce for RI (unlike CO's 24h / MD's 7-day) — but the "as soon as reasonably practicable" standard (Rules 20.39.E, 20.53.G) still implies withdrawals shouldn't be artificially batched/delayed; verify RI isn't inheriting a different state's numeric SLA constant by mistake.
- Funding-method allowlist for RI should map to exactly: debit instrument (OSB) / debit or prepaid-access instrument (iGaming), plus ACH/electronic transfer, plus "other Division-approved" — confirm credit-card deposit paths are gated off (or explicitly Division-approved) for RI specifically, per product.
- $20 minimum / $10,000 daily maximum deposit cap for OSB (Rule 20.38.A) — check this is enforced as a per-day rolling cap on the RI account, distinct from any per-transaction limit.
- No P2P account-to-account transfer for RI Online Gaming/iGaming Accounts (Rules 20.36.B, 20.50.B) — verify transfer/gift endpoints reject RI-flagged accounts as a destination or source between two different Players.
- 16-month suspension / 3-year dormant-to-unclaimed-property lifecycle (Rules 20.36.C–D, 20.50.C–D; R.I. Gen. Laws §33-21.1-1 et seq.) — confirm account-lifecycle jobs implement both thresholds and that dormant-fund escheatment routes to RI's unclaimed-property process rather than a generic fallback.
- Self-exclusion must trigger forfeiture-to-Division of any winnings net of tax withholding (Rule 20.4.B), not a return-to-patron flow (contrast MD, which requires returning balance to a self-excluded bettor) — a state comparison table that assumes uniform self-exclusion payout handling will get RI wrong.
- 5-year account-record retention (Rules 20.36.F, 20.50.F) and mandatory encryption in transit/at rest (Rules 20.40.C, 20.54.C) — confirm RI-specific data lifecycle/retention config meets or exceeds 5 years and that PII fields are covered by at-rest encryption, not just in-transit.
- W-2G-triggering payouts must run the RI Family Court child-support setoff check before disbursement (Rule 20.7) — verify the payout pipeline has a jurisdiction-gated setoff hook for RI (this is a Casino Act-wide requirement, not limited to sports/iGaming, so check whether the existing VLT/table-games code path already has this hook and whether OSB/iGaming payout code reuses it).
- No player-funds reserve calculation to build for RI (unlike CO/MD) — do not silently apply a CO/MD-style reserve-adequacy check to RI; if a shared reserve module exists, confirm it's not incorrectly gated to include RI.
- Track the pending second-operator (Bally's) launch (no earlier than 11/26/2026): a second RI OSB integration is coming with its own KYC/payments stack — confirm any "one operator per state" assumptions in routing/config don't silently break when a second RI operator activates.
