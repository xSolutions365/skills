# Alberta — Alberta Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis (AGLC) / Alberta iGaming Corporation (AiGC)

**Code:** AB · **Country: CANADA — not a US state** · **Products:** sportsbook + iGaming (casino, poker, live dealer, DFS treated as iGaming); **iBingo not approved**, iLottery reserved to Play Alberta/WCLC, political & election betting banned (SRIG 4.6.5 p)) · **Key authority:** *iGaming Alberta Act* (Bill 48, SA 2025 c I-0.2) + *Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis Act* / *Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis Regulation* (GLCR ss. 34.1–34.3, Schedule 1.1); **Standards and Requirements for Internet Gaming (SRIG)** — issued 13 Jan 2026, amended 5 Feb 2026 (s. 5.1), current version dated 18 Jun 2026, 88 pp., 5 sections (1 General Information, 2 Regulatory Oversight, 3 Social Responsibility, 4 General Standards for Registered iGaming Suppliers, 5 IT & Security); companion instruments: **Notification Matrix** (eff. 30 Apr 2026), **iGaming Compliance Approach** (eff. 30 Apr 2026), **Internet Gaming Go-Live Compliance Guide** (eff. 17 Mar 2026), **iGaming Compliance Guide FAQs** (2026-05-15), Self-Exclusion Standard & Requirements for iGaming Operators (Jul 2026), Fee Schedule; **AiGC Operating Agreement + AiGC Privacy Directive + AiGC API data specifications (all NOT public)**

> **Use `references/canada-federal-baseline.md` as this lane's federal layer, NOT `federal-baseline.md`.** There is no BSA, no FinCEN CTR/SAR, no OFAC in Alberta. The reporting entity regime is the **PCMLTFA and FINTRAC**, and SRIG 4.4.4 f) writes PCMLTFA compliance directly into the registration data set. Applying the US federal baseline here produces false green ratings on AML and sanctions. **Alberta also diverges from Ontario on privacy, but not in the way you would expect:** Alberta does have a substantially-similar private-sector statute (**PIPA**) that displaces PIPEDA for intra-provincial private-sector activity — but per AiGC's Privacy Directive the statute governing *core iGaming data* is Alberta's **public-sector** *Protection of Privacy Act* (**POPA**, in force 2025) plus the *Access to Information Act* (ATIA), because operators act as agents of AiGC. **PIPA is the residual, not the baseline.** See "Records, reporting & data" — this contradicts the common "Alberta = PIPA" shorthand and is the most likely privacy mis-scoping in a codebase built for Ontario.
>
> **Market maturity warning:** Alberta went live **13 July 2026 with 22 registered iGaming sites** (~55+ operator sites had expressed interest; roughly 50 registrants across operator and supplier categories). This file is written five weeks after launch. Practical consequences for a code review: (1) **there is no enforcement history** — no AGLC notices of administrative sanction against an iGaming registrant, so no interpretive gloss on any standard; (2) **the standards are still moving** — the SRIG has been amended at least twice in seven months (5 Feb 2026 security addendum; a 18 Jun 2026 reissue) and AGLC's own FAQ was reissued 15 May 2026 reversing its position on ID inspection at registration; (3) the substantive commercial and data terms sit in the **non-public AiGC Operating Agreement and Privacy Directive**, so silence in the SRIG is not permission. (4) **The specific failure mode to hunt for: Alberta implemented as a copy of the Ontario build.** Every operator in this market with an Ontario licence shipped Alberta as a fast follow, and the divergences are exactly the kind that a config flag misses — age **18 not 19**, CSE is **AGLC's own registry, not BetGuard**, self-exclusion terms are **6 months–3 years not 6 months–5 years**, breaks in play are **1 day/1 week/1 month/2 month/3 month** (same) but limit reminders have **mandated frequencies** Ontario does not impose, wallets **cannot be shared across provinces**, and Ontario's prepaid-card cap has **no Alberta counterpart on the public record**. A jurisdiction-agnostic `CANADA` branch is a defect here, not a shortcut.

## Payments-relevant requirements

### Licensing & change management

- **Two gates, both mandatory.** AGLC registration establishes eligibility and technical compliance; the **AiGC Operating Agreement** confers commercial authority to operate. AGLC's own guidance is explicit that registration alone does not authorise gaming operations and that AiGC "controls the final operational switch". AGLC's split: *"Commercial agreements, anti-money laundering, financials and reporting income fall under AiGC's purview."* AGLC keeps registration, standards, compliance and centralized self-exclusion integration.
- **Registration is per iGaming site.** Each distinct site requires a separate application and a separate annual fee: **CAD 50,000 one-time application fee + CAD 150,000 annual per site**. Suppliers: **CAD 15,000/yr** platform providers and critical-gaming-systems providers; **CAD 3,000/yr** "supplier-other" — a category that **expressly names e-wallet providers**, oddsmakers, Independent Integrity Monitors and ATFs. No supplier application fee published. **A payment/wallet vendor in this stack is very likely a registrable Alberta supplier in its own right** — check whether the client's PSPs and wallet provider hold AGLC registration.
- Economics (context for GGR/settlement code): 2% of GGR to First Nations + 1% of GGR to social responsibility come off the top, then net iGaming revenue splits **80% operator / 20% government** via AiGC (~22.4% effective provincial take vs Ontario's ~20% of NGR).
- **ATF certification scope is genuinely ambiguous in Alberta and this is the highest-value finding for a payments review.** SRIG **4.12.1** says an AGLC-registered ATF "must be used for compliance testing and certification of components, **including gaming platforms and all software**" — language materially broader than Ontario, where platforms/PAM/payments/wallets are expressly *out* of ITL scope. But SRIG **4.12.2** immediately narrows the substance: *"The scope of the certification is not 'all' Standards, but rather those Standards that are relevant to games, random number generators, remote gaming servers and Sport and Event Betting systems being tested."* **Read together, payment rails, wallets and PAM are almost certainly outside substantive certification scope, as in Ontario — but Alberta has not said so, and 4.12.1's "gaming platforms and all software" wording is a live exposure.** Do not rate this green on the Ontario analogy. Treat as **not verified**; escalate to AGLC iGaming Compliance (MEDIUM that the practical scope matches Ontario).
- Change management for certified components, SRIG **4.12.7 b)** — three buckets, identical in shape to Ontario's ITL Certification Policy: **Non-Regulatory** (no recertification; supplier confirms the delta is non-regulatory), **Regulatory** (must be certified *before* deployment), **Regulatory Fix / Emergency Fix** (may deploy immediately but **must be submitted to an ATF for Alberta certification within five business days of release**). **4.12.6** — recertification required where any modification, or a subsequently discovered undetected issue, impacts critical gaming system integrity, fairness, security or SRIG/GLCA/GLCR compliance; the prior certification is thereby rendered invalid. **4.12.8** retain testing/certification records and produce to AGLC on request. **4.12.9** no certification contingent on future changes.
- **Ontario certifications are portable only briefly.** AGLC will accept AGCO game certifications where they correspond to what was approved and implemented in Ontario *and* the issuing ATF is fully registered in Alberta — but **after 90 days post-launch (i.e. from ~11 Oct 2026) all game certifications must be Alberta-certified and filed with iGaming Compliance** (FAQ). ATFs must add the SRIG to their ISO accreditation scope within one year of Alberta registration.
- **Control Activity Matrix (CAM)** — summary of all iGaming-site controls **including controls performed by third-party platform providers**, supported by an **independent audit** performed by a function not involved in developing the CAM (internal audit or designated external auditor). Timing is risk-based: elevated-risk operators (new to regulated iGaming, no prior licences, history of non-compliance) may be required to submit **before** go-live; operators already licensed elsewhere generally get **up to three months post go-live**, confirmed by AGLC in writing (FAQ). A standards **gap analysis** is also required. SRIG **4.11.4** note: AGLC may **direct third-party audits** which, although retained and paid for by the registrant, **report directly to AGLC**.
- **Annual Technology Compliance Confirmation** covering the entire Alberta technology solution — platform, infrastructure, network devices, OS, databases, gaming software and other applications — plus the **integration layer** for third-party supplier systems even where the supplier files its own confirmation (MEDIUM: secondary source; scope wording not verified against the SRIG text). Payment integrations sit inside that integration layer.
- Security assurance, SRIG **5.1.6** as amended 5 Feb 2026: **5.1.6 a) i) — SOC 2 Type 1 attestation required at market launch, prior to going live**, for every iGaming site named on the registration; **5.1.6 b) — ISO 27001 certification *or* SOC 2 Type 2 attestation *or* an AGLC-approved equivalent within two years after market launch** (i.e. by ~13 Jul 2028). Obtaining Type 2 or ISO 27001 pre-launch waives the Type 1 requirement. **All required *and* optional SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria must be in scope.** An Agreed-Upon-Procedures report is **not** acceptable; GSF 1 / GSF 2 / GSF 5 are **not** acceptable equivalents. Attestation firms must be independent CPA firms licensed in a US state or Canadian province and subject to AICPA peer review or equivalent provincial practice inspection. **An Ontario SOC 2 Type 1 is only valid in Alberta if the Alberta environment is demonstrably identical in scope, architecture, controls and operating model** — a shared multi-tenant Canadian platform will need an explicit Alberta scope statement. Attestations are required **at operator level only**; third-party goods/services suppliers and subservice organisations are not currently required to produce their own (FAQ) — though SRIG 4.11.11 still makes the operator answerable for them.
- **Penetration testing before market launch and annually thereafter**, covering application layer **and** the operator's own internal and external network layers (FAQ).
- SRIG **5.5.16 b)** — all third-party-developed code must pass **integration testing** before promotion to production; an ATF is **not** required to perform that testing (FAQ).
- SRIG **4.11.11** — registrants are responsible for the actions of third parties they contract with for any aspect of the Alberta gaming business and must require them to conduct themselves "as if they were bound by the same laws, regulations and standards". This reaches PSPs, wallet vendors, KYC vendors and geolocation vendors. **4.11.13** — no affiliate or referral third party paid by the operator may also perform those activities for unregistered sites accepting Alberta wagers.
- Data residency: **AGLC does not require data to be stored in Canada**, but storage or processing outside Canada triggers a **review and approval process, and some countries may not be approved** (FAQ). Data centres and remote gaming servers require AGLC approval before operations commence, covering data residency, cross-border transfer assessment and **encryption key residency** (MEDIUM: secondary source for the three-element breakdown).

### Deposits & permitted payment methods

- **Cryptocurrency is banned.** Note to SRIG **4.4.23**: *"Cryptocurrency is not legal tender and must not be accepted."* Same substance and near-identical wording to Ontario's note to standard 5.69.
- **Credit cards are permitted and there is no Alberta credit-card restriction.** SRIG **3.2.1** prohibits the operator or its employees from extending credit in any form, lending money to players, referring players to credit providers, or inferring that a player should seek additional credit to play. SRIG **3.2.2** then says expressly: *"The prohibition at 3.2.1 does not prohibit a player from using a credit card issued in their name by a financial institution as a form of payment."* Two code-relevant consequences: card funding must be allowed, **and the card must be in the player's own name issued by a financial institution** — so a card-name/account-holder match check is a standards requirement, not just fraud hygiene. A gateway that ports a US card-funding ban into Alberta is over-restricting.
- **No prepaid-card or e-money-voucher limit is published for Alberta, and none was located in any form.** Ontario's non-public CAD 250 per rolling 7 days / 5 instruments per 7 days rule (recovered from an internal operator engineering requirement labelled "Regulation 8.1 — Deposit") has **no counterpart in the SRIG, the FAQs, the Go-Live Compliance Guide, the Notification Matrix or the Compliance Approach**, and nothing equivalent appears in any AGLC or AiGC publication. Because Ontario's cap is itself a non-public iGO requirement, **the realistic possibility is that an equivalent lives in the non-public AiGC Operating Agreement**. Status: **not verified — treat as an open question, not as an absence.** Do not code Alberta as "no prepaid cap" on the strength of public silence, and do not code Ontario's CAD 250/5 into Alberta either. Ask AiGC.
- **Deposits must be verified by an authorized financial services provider** before funds may be credited — SRIG **4.4.23 a)**.
- **Funds must not be transferred between player accounts** — SRIG **4.4.29**, absolute, with no approval carve-out. Player-to-player transfers, gifting and bonus-shifting between accounts are prohibited.
- **Third parties are not permitted to access a player's account** — SRIG **4.4.12**; players must be offered the *option* of multi-factor authentication at login (4.4.12 Req 1 — offered, not mandatory).
- **One player account per gaming site** (**4.4.9**); all accounts uniquely identifiable (**4.4.8**); all account transactions uniquely identifiable and traceable to a single account (**4.4.16**); all account transactions recorded and logged accurately and completely (**4.4.13**).
- **No negative balance** — SRIG **4.4.25**: no account may have a negative funds balance and *"no bet will be accepted that could result in a negative funds balance."* Note Alberta omits Ontario 5.75's express account-suspension mechanic and Ontario's resettlement guidance; the prohibition is bare.
- **Currency: the player balance must be displayed in Canadian dollars** — SRIG **4.4.26** Req 1. Net position for slot sessions must also be displayed in CAD (**4.10.11**), and AGLC has confirmed 4.10.11 applies equally to **instant games** notwithstanding that the standard says "slots" (FAQ).
- **Fee disclosure before the transaction** — SRIG **4.4.27**: unambiguous information about all player account fees *prior to* making a withdrawal or deposit. **4.4.28** — players must be informed clearly and specifically of all rules and restrictions on deposits, withdrawals and access to funds.
- **Cross-province wallet sharing is prohibited**: *"No, distinct websites between provinces will require separate e-wallets"* (FAQ, Player Wallets). An Alberta player cannot share a wallet with the operator's Ontario site. If the codebase has a single Canadian wallet entity keyed on customer rather than on site/province, that is a defect.
- Player-visible transaction data must include, per SRIG **4.4.15**: deposit and withdrawal history and current balance; **method and source of funds used for transactions**; last login date/time; gaming and transaction history including bet placement and settlement times for Sports and Event Betting; monies wagered/won/lost per session or period; and balance at session start and end.
- **Adjustments**: only by authorised individuals (**4.4.30**), recorded and logged accurately and completely (**4.4.31**), with accurate, clear and specific reasons given to the player (**4.4.32**).
- No Alberta-specific rule was located for Interac e-Transfer, Interac Online, eCash/PayNearMe-style rails, ACH/EFT or open banking. They are permitted under the general 4.4.23 + AML frame. Interac remains the dominant Canadian rail in both directions.

### Withdrawals & payout timelines

- **There is no numeric withdrawal SLA in Alberta.** SRIG **4.4.24**: operators must ensure players receive withdrawal funds *"in a reasonable and timely manner"*, and withdrawals are permitted *"in an accurate and complete fashion and as soon as is practicable, subject to appropriate authorization and verification."* Same outcome-based drafting as Ontario 5.71 — the testable clock is whatever the operator publishes, not a regulatory figure. Do not import Michigan's 10 business days or Kansas's 5 calendar days.
- **Closed-loop / name-match verification is mandatory before payout** — SRIG **4.4.23 b)**: before a withdrawal is permitted, it must be verified and authorised to ensure (i) *"the withdrawal is being made by a holder of the account"* and (ii) *"the withdrawal is being transferred to an account of which the player is a legal holder."* Interac e-Transfer to a non-matching name or email is the classic breach.
- **Verification precedes the first deposit as well as the first withdrawal** — 4.4.23 gates both directions, and 4.4.4 requires validated identity **before the account is created at all** (see KYC below). Alberta therefore does not have a "deposit now, verify later" window on the face of the standards.
- **Withholding for investigation** is contemplated in SRIG s. 4.3 (unfair behaviour, cheating and collusion — subsection number **not verified**): complaints must be investigated; the operator's policies on deterring, preventing and detecting cheating and collusion **including suspension or disabling of accounts and any recovery of funds** must be publicly available on request; where an investigation results in suspension or disabling of an account the investigation records, reason, and evidence must be retained per **4.16.1**; and AGLC must be informed via the **Notification Matrix** of any incident the operator reasonably believes constitutes cheating. Unlike Ontario 4.33, **no express requirement that the operator "must ensure it has reserved itself the authority"** in its T&Cs was located — but the T&C contract requirement at 4.4.11 makes the same practical point.
- **Reverse withdrawals / withdrawal cancellation are not addressed anywhere in the located Alberta material.** Do not assert an Alberta restriction and do not assume the Ontario position (also unregulated) transfers by analogy. **Not verified**; possible OA or RG Check exposure (LOW).
- **Balance recovery rights**: dormant or player-deactivated accounts — the player must be able to recover the balance owing (**4.4.22**); reasonable efforts must be made to inform players of funds remaining in dormant accounts (**4.4.17**); players may deactivate at any time and the account is then deactivated (**4.4.18**); the operator may deactivate where necessary (**4.4.19**); **an account must be deactivated if requested by AGLC *or* AiGC** (**4.4.20**) — note the dual-trigger, which needs two authorised external channels into account state.
- **Self-exclusion payout**: SRIG **3.5.3 e)** — a mechanism to facilitate return of the balance of unused funds, when requested, to a self-excluding player. **3.5.4** — once a player self-excludes, *"the wager is brought to an end."* Alberta states this flatly; contrast Ontario's more elaborate 24-hour cancel-and-refund window with a carve-out for events commencing within 24 hours. **Alberta's drafting is terser and appears to admit no such carve-out** — a build that reuses Ontario's 24-hour/pre-commencement logic may under-perform Alberta (MEDIUM).
- **Player-account change notification is unusually prescriptive and directly hits payout plumbing** — SRIG **4.4.33**: for changes to contact details, credentials, authentication factors, **payout/banking information** or security settings, notification must go to the **last known verified contact point(s) on file** (e.g. previous email and/or phone) and *"do not rely solely on the newly provided contact point"*; send via **more than one channel where feasible** (e.g. email plus SMS/push); notification content **must avoid clickable links** and must instruct the player to sign in directly; include the **date/time** of the change and, where permitted, **masked device/IP** information; and log and retain all notifications and follow-up actions per **4.16.1**. **Ontario has no equivalent standard.** This is the single most likely Alberta-specific gap in an Ontario-derived codebase, and it is greppable.

### Player funds segregation / reserve

- **The SRIG imposes no trust account, no segregation formula and no reserve calculation on the operator.** SRIG **4.4.34** says only: *"Player funds must be clearly and appropriately managed."* That is the whole operator-side duty on the face of the standards — functionally identical to Ontario 5.72.
- **The substantive duty is placed on the conducting entity, not the operator** — SRIG **4.4.35**: all player funds deposited in respect of iGaming lottery schemes **conducted and managed by AGLC** must be held in an **AGLC designated account**; and *"**AiGC must take steps to ensure that all player funds deposited in respect of iGaming lottery schemes conducted and managed by AiGC are subject to oversight by AiGC and available to players.**"* This is the Ontario 5.73 pattern transplanted verbatim in structure.
- **Practical consequence for the review:** as in Ontario, a reviewer expecting an NJ/PA/MI-style reserve computation will find nothing to test, and that absence is **not** an operator defect. The testable mechanics — designated/segregated accounts, performance security, GGR-versus-player-funds separation, controls over wallets and unsettled wagers, reporting cadence — will be in the **non-public AiGC Operating Agreement**, by direct analogy to iGO's performance security and its OA s. 10.01 Report on Key Internal Controls. **Nothing about the AiGC OA's financial-security or player-funds terms was locatable publicly (LOW).** *iGaming Alberta Act* s. 14(1) does require AiGC to ensure that anyone it contracts with makes reports, accounts, records and other documents available to AiGC immediately on request — which is the statutory hook for whatever the OA imposes.
- Do not rate green on SRIG silence here. Rate as **evidence-gap / ask the client for the AiGC OA and any AiGC financial reporting policy**.

### Responsible gambling

- **Limits are offered, not defaulted — no mandatory default cap exists in Alberta.** SRIG **3.3.8** requires system-enforced controls: **a)** time limits at registration or any time after, restricted in increments of **one hour** minimum, measured as time spent in areas of the site where games may be played; **b)** **loss limits and deposit limits** at registration or any time after — deposit limit = amount deposited over a player-chosen period; loss limit = amount lost (winnings less amount spent). Periods offered **must include 24 hours, 7 days and one month**. *"Where a player sets simultaneous periods (e.g., a deposit limit for a day and for a week), **the lowest limit must apply**."* Limit functions must be easy to find, easy to initiate and easy to change at any time post-registration. **Note the drafting is "system enforced"** — a customer-service-mediated limit is non-compliant.
- **Timing asymmetry** — SRIG **3.3.11**: a request to **relax or eliminate** a previously established limit must **a)** only be made by the player, and **b)** only be implemented after a **cooling-off period of at least 24 hours**. No waiting period is prescribed for a **decrease** or a new stricter limit, so it should take effect immediately — that is an inference from the drafting, not an express statement (MEDIUM). Same 24-hour number as Ontario 2.24; the same code path likely satisfies both.
- **Reminders have mandated frequencies in Alberta and do not in Ontario** — SRIG **3.3.10**: periodic reminders to review limit-setting and account activity, at minimum **quarterly for time and finance-based limits** and **monthly for reminders to review financial activity**. Ontario 2.23/2.24 impose no frequency. **Greppable Alberta-only obligation.**
- **Break in play** — SRIG **3.3.9**: must be offered *in addition to* AGLC's self-exclusion program, in terms of **one day, one week, one month, two months, three months**; no further wagers during the break; **during a short-term break the player may still access account balance, profile and RG features** (so a break must not block cashout or limit-setting — a fail-closed session lock that blanks the wallet is non-compliant).
- **Centralized self-exclusion is AGLC-run and was a day-one launch gate — this is the sharpest Alberta/Ontario divergence.** GLCR **s. 34.2** + SRIG **3.5.1**: operators must not permit a person enrolled in **AGLC's** self-exclusion program to enter or remain on the site. SRIG **3.5.2 a)**: operators must have an **effective API connection** (security per **5.1.6**) to AGLC's centralized system for self-excluded persons, with **effective controls preventing any individual not cleared by AGLC's centralized system from registering an account or logging into an existing account**. SRIG **3.5.3**: promote AGLC's SE program and RG materials; allow players to access the **centralized SE tool** through the site; controls preventing self-excluded players from accessing the account or gaming; exclude self-excluded players from **all** marketing once notified; and the unused-funds return mechanism at 3.5.3 e).
  - Mechanics: sign-up is **online-only at selfexclusion.ca**, requiring name exactly as on government ID, supplemental data (DOB, height, weight), a **portrait/selfie photo upload**, and T&C agreement. Three scopes: **all registered iGaming; all land-based casinos and racing entertainment centres; or both.** Terms run **six months to three years**. AGLC IT initiates CSE onboarding once the application materials are in and fees are paid; **pre-registered patrons are retro-screened against the CSE database once the program becomes active** (FAQ).
- **Prohibited-persons registry is a *second*, separate AGLC API** — SRIG **3.4.1**: AGLC maintains a centralized list of persons **convicted or legally excluded** under GLCR **ss. 34.1 and 34.3**, or otherwise determined inadmissible. **3.4.2 a)**: effective API connection to that system with controls preventing any individual not cleared from registering or logging in. **3.4.3**: **discrepancy reports (see s. 5.7.4) must be submitted to AGLC within 72 hours** on all prohibited persons who attempt to enter or remain on the site. **3.4.4**: as AGLC updates the banned/self-excluded list, **all registered player information must be re-verified** and ineligible players prohibited from gaming — i.e. a reconciliation sweep, not just a point-of-login check. Ontario's BetGuard is a single self-exclusion feed; **Alberta expects real-time clearance against a combined self-excluded *and* legally-excluded population plus a 72-hour discrepancy report.** Note also that fail-open/fail-closed behaviour when the AGLC API is unavailable is not addressed in the located text (**not verified**) — Ontario 2.14.1 expressly requires managing registry-check disruptions, so build to the stricter reading.
- **Harm monitoring must be able to consume payment signals** — SRIG **3.3.12** (policies and program to assess and monitor player risk profiles, identifying moderate/high risk and providing assistance), **3.3.13** (effective mechanism proactively identifying at-risk players, drawing on **all available data sources** on a player-by-player basis; see Attachment 3.3), **3.3.14** (intervene in a manner that is timely and commensurate with risk, and evaluate intervention impact). Deposit velocity, failed-deposit retries, payment-method churn and loss-chasing sit squarely inside "all available data sources".
- **RG surfacing on payment screens** — SRIG **4.4.1**: the registration page **and pages within the player account** must prominently display an RG statement, the online link, and the **GameSense** number, and link to RG materials and support resources. AGLC also requires reference to **211 Alberta** in advertising. Note **GameSense is an Alberta/AGLC brand that Ontario does not use** — Ontario-derived RG components will carry the wrong branding and wrong helpline.
- **RG Check accreditation (Responsible Gambling Council) is mandatory** for every Alberta iGaming site **within two years of entering the market** (AiGC/RGC partnership announced Feb 2026). Existing Ontario RG Check accreditation streamlines but does not substitute — a distinct Alberta accreditation is required. Ontario does not mandate RG Check.
- **Advertising and inducements** — SRIG **3.3.6**: all advertising and marketing materials must contain a responsible gambling message. Inducements (bonuses, promotions, special offers) **are permitted** where they appear on the operator's website or are sent to players who gave **express, informed consent** to receive marketing, and must be clear, accurate and responsibly presented (FAQ; GLCR **Schedule 1.1**). Generic broad-reach advertising that references no specific inducement is not an inducement but still needs an RG message. Loyalty and reward programs **may** be advertised externally subject to Schedule 1.1. Athletes (active or retired) and persons appealing to minors may not endorse, except when promoting RG features. Advertising must not be intentionally communicated to self-excluded or high-risk individuals. Advertising agencies do not require registration; the registrant is accountable. **Caution:** one secondary source asserts that bonuses and sign-up offers "may not be advertised publicly" in Alberta; **AGLC's own FAQ says the opposite** for consented direct marketing and on-site presentation. Trust the FAQ.
- Fit-to-play: SRIG **4.4.7** — prior to participating in game play, players must affirm they are fit for play. Not an Ontario standard in this form.

### KYC / age / identity

- **Minimum age is 18 — confirmed.** SRIG **4.4.2 a)** makes an individual **under 18 years of age** ineligible to play; AiGC's own site footer reads "18+ Play Responsibly." **Ontario is 19.** This is the highest-frequency hard-coding risk in a Canadian build: a shared `CANADA` or `ON`-derived age gate set to 19 silently excludes lawful 18-year-old Albertans, and a global 21 (US) gate excludes far more. **4.4.3** — persons ineligible under 4.4.2 are also **ineligible for prizes**; note this contrasts with Ontario 3.01 Req 2, where self-excluded persons *remain* prize-eligible. Alberta's 4.4.2 list bundles minors, self-exclusion participants, court-order-restricted persons, legally/self-excluded prohibited persons, **officers/directors/partners of the operator**, and **employees of registered iGaming Suppliers** — and 4.4.3 strips prize eligibility from all of them.
- **Verification must complete before the account exists.** SRIG **4.4.4**: relevant player information must be collected and saved on registration and *"must be demonstrated to be complete, accurate and validated **before a player account is created** for the player."* Minimum data set: **a)** full name **exactly as it appears on FINTRAC-compliant government-issued identification** — *"Provincial healthcare cards are not an acceptable form of identification"*; **b)** date of birth matched to FINTRAC-compliant government-issued ID; **c)** **FINTRAC-compliant physical address** — *"only physical address or legal land description is acceptable"* (so no PO boxes); **d)** a method of identification for subsequent logon such as a unique username; **e)** contact information including valid email **and** phone; **f)** all information and results of reasonable measures required by the **PCMLTFA** and its regulations. Timing therefore: **verification precedes account creation, which precedes first deposit and first wager.** Combined with 4.4.23 a)/b), Alberta has no lawful pre-verification deposit window on the face of the standards.
- **AGLC reversed its position on ID inspection.** The FAQ as updated **2026-05-15** states that operators are **not** required to view government-issued photo ID at registration under 4.4.4, and that a **credit-file or dual-process method** is permitted for identity verification under FINTRAC rules. AGLC nonetheless recommends instructing players to enter their name exactly as on government ID under those alternate methods, to support file matching, avoid duplicate business relationships, and reliably identify **banned, legally excluded and self-excluded** clients. **The name-exactness requirement is doing CSE-matching work, not just KYC work** — a lenient name-normalisation layer degrades self-exclusion enforcement.
- **Geolocation as a supplemental KYC signal is expressly blessed**: where address verification returns a minor discrepancy (typo, slightly stale data), a geolocation check confirming the player is physically at or near the stated residential address may be treated as a successful verification step; the operator must then update the accurate address on the player account (FAQ).
- Supporting standards: **4.4.5** player affirms all registration information is complete and accurate before the account is created; **4.4.6** player information must be kept complete and accurate; **4.4.10** auditable, logged trail of account creation/activation/deactivation/changes including identification-and-verification information and contract-related information; **4.4.11** players must accept the account/game-play contract **before account creation** and must accept material changes **on logging in**, and the contract must at all times comply with the SRIG and applicable Alberta law; **4.4.12** all players authenticated before accessing the account and before being permitted to gamble.
- **4.4.21** — if player information is removed it must still be **retained per 4.16.1** or other applicable retention rules. A deletion request cannot purge the regulatory record.
- Pre-launch pre-registration was permitted once the operator was in a business relationship with AGLC (application filed, fees paid), with **no deposits or wagers** before go-live, and retro-screening against CSE (FAQ; AGLC/Gowling).

### Geolocation / jurisdiction

- **SRIG 4.2.1 is the only location standard and it is short but demanding.** Registered Operators must ensure games are provided **only within Alberta** unless conducted in conjunction with the government of another province. At minimum the gaming system must: **a)** *"ensure only players **physically located in Alberta** can participate and **block play when location cannot be verified**"* — i.e. **fail-closed is mandated**; **b)** *"implement controls to detect and block location-evasion methods (including known VPNs/proxies, remote desktop or virtualization, and rooted/jail-broken devices)"*; **c)** *"perform **periodic re-verification** of player location and **log each verification in a timestamped, tamper-evident manner** for audit per 4.16.1."*
- The operator's technology compliance submission must cover the whole Alberta stack **including third-party integrations** and explain how the site enforces Alberta-only play, including dynamic location monitoring and anti-VPN/proxy/RDP/virtualisation controls.
- **Scope question — does the geofence bind deposits and withdrawals, or only wagering?** 4.2.1 is framed on *games provided*, *players participating* and *blocking play*. **It does not address deposits, withdrawals or account management from outside Alberta, and no AGLC guidance on the point was located. Not verified.** Ontario permits account management (including some money movement) from outside the province; **do not assume Alberta matches.** Given 4.2.1's fail-closed drafting and the 4.4.23 verification gates, the conservative build blocks wagering hard and treats out-of-province cashout as an escalation, not a silent allow. Flag for AGLC iGaming Compliance.
- **Cross-province and cross-border peer-to-peer is unresolved.** AGLC states it is still assessing the Ontario Court of Appeal decision on international liquidity and *"no determination has been made at this time"* regarding players located outside Canada; operators should plan on Alberta's location-control rules as the baseline (FAQ). If the codebase has an Ontario international-liquidity flag, it must not default on for Alberta.
- Legally-excluded/self-excluded clearance (3.4.2, 3.5.2) is a separate gate from location and must not be collapsed into one "eligibility" call.

### AML overlays (provincial layer over the Canadian federal baseline)

- **Federal floor is unchanged**: operators are reporting entities under the **PCMLTFA**; obligations run **directly to FINTRAC** and are not displaced by the provincial structure — written compliance programme, chief AML compliance officer, documented risk assessment, scheduled training, LCTRs, EFTRs, STRs without delay, biennial independent effectiveness review. Use `canada-federal-baseline.md`.
- **The Alberta-specific twist is the reporting line.** AGLC directs **all AML and FINTRAC process inquiries to AiGC**, and the Go-Live Compliance Guide assigns **AML and financial reporting to AiGC**. AGLC's own iGaming page: AML "falls under AiGC's purview." **So Alberta adds an AML supervisor with visibility of the operator's controls plus an additional reporting cadence that Ontario does not have** — iGO does not sit between the operator and FINTRAC. This is a genuine Ontario→Alberta delta and it is an integration/reporting question, not a policy question.
- **The SRIG requires an AML/ATF program that goes beyond the federal baseline**, including risk-based escalation measures, **source-of-funds corroboration where risk requires it**, and retention of FINTRAC-filed reports (plus any copies or notifications owed to AiGC under the go-live/reporting process) so they can be made available to AGLC through the **Notification Matrix** (MEDIUM — this is a law-firm paraphrase; the specific SRIG subsection numbers were not verified against the standard text).
- Payment-adjacent AML hooks in the standards: **4.4.23** deposit verification by an authorized financial services provider + crypto ban; **4.4.15 b)** player-visible **method and source of funds**; **4.4.4 f)** PCMLTFA data captured at registration; **4.4.29** no inter-account transfers (a classic layering vector); **4.4.16** every transaction traceable to a unique account; **4.3.1** continuous independent monitoring of cash and cash-equivalent handling with SIEM correlation.
- Enforcement climate is live and Alberta-adjacent: FINTRAC penalised the Saskatchewan Indian Gaming Authority **CAD 1,175,000** (28 Aug 2025, under appeal) and Northern Isga Foundation (Eagle River Casino, Glenevis, Alberta) **CAD 91,162.50** (2 Mar 2026) for programme, risk-assessment, training and review failings. 23 Notices of Violation totalling >CAD 25m across gaming in 2024/25. Land-based cases, same federal regime.

### Records, reporting & data

- **Retention anchor is SRIG 4.16.1**, cross-referenced from at least 3.4.3-adjacent reporting, 4.3.1 (logs), 4.3.2 (transaction/game-state records), 4.4.21 (removed player information), 4.4.33 (change notifications) and the cheating-investigation subsection of 4.3. **The actual periods could not be read from the primary text** (the 88-page SRIG PDF at aglc.ca is not machine-retrievable through available tooling). A secondary index reports a two-tier structure — **event and security logs: ≥1 year online/immediately accessible then ≥7 years archived; general compliance records including SRIG- and Control-Activity-related records: ≥3 years unless a longer period is specified elsewhere** (**MEDIUM — single secondary source; verify 4.16.1 verbatim before relying on it**).
- Log integrity, SRIG **4.3.1**: continuous logs for critical gaming systems covering financial accounting and game-state history; logs protected against alteration via **WORM/immutability or cryptographic signing with SHA-256**; transmitted and stored over **TLS 1.2+**; access **role-based with segregation of duties between operations and monitoring**; retained per 4.16.1 and produced to AGLC on request; **SIEM-style monitoring function** correlating and alerting on integrity events with remediation tracked to closure. **4.3.2** — accurate, complete transaction and game-state records for AGLC investigations, partial-game continuation, dispute and complaint resolution, and tracking of player funds information.
- **Reporting channel is the Notification Matrix (eff. 30 Apr 2026)** — it defines what registrants must send AGLC, at what frequency and in what format, including **discrepancy reporting** and regulatory submissions. Named triggers located: prohibited-person access attempts (**72 hours**, 3.4.3 → 5.7.4); suspected cheating incidents (4.3, via the Matrix); player complaints about account, **funds management** or game play (**4.11.8** — the complaint mechanism must be timely and AGLC must be notified per the Matrix). **The Matrix PDF itself was not retrievable; treat its full trigger list as an evidence gap.**
- Audit rights: **4.11.4** note — AGLC may direct third-party audits which report **directly to AGLC** though retained by the registrant; CAM must be independently audited; annual Technology Compliance Confirmation; **iGaming Alberta Act s. 14(1)** obliges AiGC to secure immediate on-request access to reports, accounts, records and documents from anyone it contracts with. Registration applicants also post an initial **CAD 10,000** background-check deposit under GLCR s. 13.2.
- **Privacy — this is where the Ontario/Alberta shorthand breaks, and it cuts against the "PIPA" assumption.** Alberta does have **PIPA**, deemed substantially similar to PIPEDA, and PIPA generally governs intra-provincial private-sector personal information — so PIPEDA is displaced for ordinary Alberta private-sector activity. **But the AiGC Privacy Directive provides that personal information collected, used or processed by operators *pursuant to their AiGC operating agreements* is governed by Alberta's public-sector *Protection of Privacy Act* (POPA)**, in force 2025, because operators act **as agents of AiGC**. **PIPA's role is residual** — operator employee data in Alberta, loyalty-programme data, anything outside the OA. Player **access and correction rights run under POPA and the *Access to Information Act* (ATIA), not PIPA**, and an operator receiving a request for information under AiGC's control **must redirect it to AiGC**. For core iGaming data flows, **Alberta is a POPA market, not a PIPA market.**
  - POPA-driven obligations with build consequences: **privacy impact assessment before go-live and before any new practice, program, project or service involving personal information**; publish a **directory of personal information banks**; a privacy management program (designated privacy officer, incident and complaint policies, security classification system, mandatory employee training, periodic reviews); and execution of a formal **POPA delegation instrument before go-live**. None of these have PIPA analogues.
  - Data-use gatekeeping: **sale of personal information is expressly prohibited** by statute and extended contractually to POPA Data; AiGC has **"exclusive and unfettered" control over Gaming Data**; operators may **not disclose, share or otherwise make available any Gaming Data without AiGC's written consent**; use is confined to delivering the operator website/offerings and performing operator obligations; where AiGC consents to further use the operator must then obtain player consent under the Privacy Directive and POPA regulations, and **AiGC does not authorise reliance on oral consent**. SRIG also requires that player personal information be used only for the lottery schemes conducted and managed by AGLC or AiGC (MEDIUM, secondary paraphrase). **POPA is new and largely untested** — little regulatory guidance, no judicial interpretation, and its interaction with the OA and Privacy Directive is unsettled.
  - **Residual PIPA breach standard (for the slice PIPA still governs):** PIPA **s. 34.1** — an organisation must, **without unreasonable delay**, notify the **Commissioner** of any loss of, or unauthorised access to or disclosure of, personal information under its control where a reasonable person would consider there exists a **real risk of significant harm** (RROSH) to an individual. PIPA **s. 37.1** — **the Commissioner then decides** and **may require** the organisation to notify affected individuals, in a form and manner prescribed by regulation and **within a time period the Commissioner determines**; **s. 37.1(7)** preserves the organisation's ability to notify on its own initiative. **How this differs from PIPEDA:** PIPEDA s. 10.1 is self-executing — the organisation reports to the OPC *and* notifies affected individuals itself, "as soon as feasible", on the same RROSH trigger. **Under PIPA, individual notification is regulator-gated**, and OIPC Alberta's practical expectation for its own notification is weeks, not months. PIPA has required mandatory breach reporting since 2010, eight years ahead of PIPEDA. **POPA's own breach-reporting standard and timing were not verified** and should not be assumed to match either PIPA or PIPEDA — this matters because POPA, not PIPA, governs the player data in scope.

## Ontario contrasts (read before reusing any Ontario finding)

1. **Age: Alberta 18, Ontario 19** (SRIG 4.4.2 a) vs Ontario). A shared Canadian age constant is a defect. Alberta also strips prize eligibility from all ineligible categories including self-excluded persons (4.4.3), whereas Ontario 3.01 Req 2 keeps self-excluded persons prize-eligible.
2. **Self-exclusion mechanism: two different registries with different owners, scopes and terms.** Alberta = **AGLC's own centralized program** (selfexclusion.ca; live for land-based, extended to iGaming from day one; scopes iGaming-only / land-based-only / both; **6 months–3 years**; selfie + physical-descriptor enrolment). Ontario = **BetGuard**, run by iGO, live only from **14 May 2026**, online-only, **6 months–5 years**, iGaming-only. Different API, different term enum, different scope model. **Alberta additionally requires a second API to a *prohibited persons* registry (convicted/legally excluded, GLCR ss. 34.1/34.3) with a 72-hour discrepancy report (SRIG 3.4.2, 3.4.3) and a re-verification sweep of all registered players on list update (3.4.4).** Ontario has no equivalent combined feed. Conversely Ontario's 2.14.1 has explicit timing mechanics (1-hour iGO add, 24-hour marketing stop, 24-hour wager cancel-and-refund with a pre-commencement carve-out, express registry-outage handling) that Alberta's 3.5.x does **not** state — Alberta just says "the wager is brought to an end" (3.5.4). Do not assume Alberta's terser text is more permissive.
3. **Prepaid cards: Ontario has a hard numeric cap (CAD 250 per rolling 7 days, 5 instruments per 7 days, per the non-public iGO "Regulation 8.1 — Deposit"); Alberta has no published equivalent — and the absence is unverified, not confirmed.** Ontario's cap is itself non-public, so the Alberta counterpart may sit in the AiGC OA. Do not port Ontario's numbers into Alberta and do not code Alberta as uncapped.
4. **Withdrawal SLA: neither province has one** (SRIG 4.4.24 ≈ Ontario 5.71). Same finding, and in both cases the enforceable clock is the operator's published standard. Do not import US clocks into either.
5. **Certification scope: Ontario expressly excludes platforms/PAM/payments/wallets/RG controls from ITL certification (Go-Live Compliance Guide s. 2). Alberta says an ATF "must be used for compliance testing and certification of components, including gaming platforms and all software" (SRIG 4.12.1) while narrowing substantive scope to games/RNG/RGS/Sports & Event Betting (4.12.2).** Practically probably the same outcome; textually not the same, and Alberta has published no exclusion list. **Highest-value open question in this file.** Change-management buckets and the 5-business-day emergency-fix window are the same in both (SRIG 4.12.7 b) ≈ Ontario Appendix C).
6. **Privacy statute: not "PIPEDA in Ontario vs PIPA in Alberta".** Both are hybrid public-sector-controlled models. Ontario = PIPEDA + FIPPA-facing iGO requirements. Alberta = **POPA + ATIA + AiGC Privacy Directive**, with PIPA residual and PIPEDA relevant only to cross-border/interprovincial flows. Alberta adds PIAs before go-live and before any new PI-involving initiative, a published personal-information-bank directory, a privacy management program, a POPA delegation instrument, and a statutory + contractual **ban on selling personal information**. If the codebase's Canadian privacy module is labelled PIPEDA or PIPA and reused for Alberta, the notices, the access-request routing and the consent basis are all wrong.
7. **Security certification: Alberta hard-codes a timeline Ontario does not** — SOC 2 Type 1 at launch, SOC 2 Type 2 or ISO 27001 within two years (SRIG 5.1.6 a) i), b)), all TSC in scope, AUP reports rejected, and an **Ontario SOC 2 Type 1 is only reusable if the Alberta environment is demonstrably identical**. Pen test pre-launch and annually including the operator's own network layers.
8. **Alberta-only operator obligations with no Ontario analogue:** mandatory **RG Check** accreditation within two years; **mandated RG reminder frequencies** (quarterly limits / monthly financial activity, SRIG 3.3.10); **fit-to-play affirmation** before game play (4.4.7); the **prescriptive account-change notification rules** at 4.4.33 (last-known-verified contact point, multi-channel, no clickable links, masked device/IP, logged); **GameSense** branding and **211 Alberta** in advertising; **political/election betting banned** (4.6.5 p)) where Ontario permits novelty political markets; **iBingo not approved**; **AML/financial reporting routed through AiGC** in addition to FINTRAC.
9. **Wallets cannot be shared across provinces** (FAQ) — an Ontario/Alberta shared wallet entity is non-compliant. Related: one account per gaming site (4.4.9), no inter-account transfers (4.4.29), third parties may not access accounts (4.4.12).
10. **Geolocation: Ontario permits account management from outside the province; Alberta has not said.** SRIG 4.2.1 mandates fail-closed blocking of *play* when location cannot be verified, plus VPN/proxy/RDP/virtualisation/rooted-device detection and **periodic re-verification with timestamped tamper-evident logs**. Whether deposits/withdrawals are inside the fence is **not verified** — do not copy Ontario's answer across.
11. **Fees and per-site structure differ** (AB CAD 50k once + CAD 150k/site/yr, ~22.4% effective take, 2% First Nations + 1% social responsibility off GGR; ON CAD 100k/site/yr, ~20% NGR) — matters for revenue-share and settlement code, not just finance.
12. **Registration gate ordering differs**: Alberta's third registration prong is **self-exclusion integration**, and AGLC treats it as a hard launch gate. Ontario's BetGuard integration only became mandatory four years after market open.

11. Limit-setting engine: verify deposit **and** loss **and** time limits exist; periods include **24h / 7d / 1 month**; **lowest limit wins** on overlap (3.3.8 b) iii)); increases/removals gated by a **≥24h cooling-off** and decreases apply immediately (3.3.11); enforcement is **system-side**, not CS-mediated. Grep `coolingOff`, `limitChangeDelay`, `24`, `pendingLimitIncrease`, `lowestLimit`.

16. Verification ordering: trace registration → KYC → account creation → first deposit → first wager.
