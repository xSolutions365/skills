# Ontario — Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario (AGCO) / iGaming Ontario (iGO)

**Code:** ON · **Country: CANADA — not a US state** · **Products:** sportsbook + iGaming · **Key authority:** Gaming Control Act, 1992 (GCA ss. 3.8, 3.9) + O. Reg. 78/12; **Registrar's Standards for Internet Gaming** (in force 4 Apr 2022, amended through May 2026 — the operative instrument, cited below as bare standard numbers); iGaming Ontario Act, 2024 (in force 12 May 2025); iGO Operating Agreement (**not public**)

> **Use `references/canada-federal-baseline.md` as this lane's federal layer, NOT `federal-baseline.md`.** There is no BSA, no FinCEN CTR/SAR, and no OFAC in Ontario. Applying the US federal baseline here produces false green ratings on the AML and sanctions areas. The Canadian file also carries a "US-control mapping traps" section written for exactly this codebase — read it.
>
> **If Alberta is also in scope, read `ab.md`'s "Ontario contrasts" section before reusing anything from this file.** Alberta launched 13 July 2026 on a deliberately Ontario-like model, which makes cross-application tempting and wrong: age is 18 not 19, self-exclusion is AGLC's own registry rather than BetGuard with different term lengths, the prepaid cap below has no published Alberta counterpart, and the privacy statute differs. Findings do not transfer between the two provinces without checking.
>
> **Structural point that changes who owes what:** AGCO regulates; **iGO conducts and manages** and is the commercial counterparty. iGO was *separated out of* AGCO on 12 May 2025 (not merged in). Market entry is two-gate: AGCO registration does not confer authority to operate — that comes from the iGO Operating Agreement, which imposes obligations *additional to* the Standards and is not public. Where the Standards are silent (player-funds segregation, withdrawal SLAs), the obligation may well live in the OA. Say so rather than rating green on silence.

## Payments-relevant requirements

### Licensing & change management

- **Platforms — including player account management, payments, player wallets and RG controls — do NOT require ITL certification** (Go-Live Compliance Guide, s. 2). This is the single most important scope fact for a payment gateway review and differs from GLI-19 states like MI.
- ITL certification scope is games, RNGs, and components that accept/process/determine/display/log **bets**, including subsequent modifications (4.08 [amended Apr 2023], 4.09 Req 1). AGCO does **not** adopt GLI-19 as the legal test; certification is against the AGCO Standards, and GLI is merely one registered ITL.
- Post-launch changes to certified components, per the ITL Certification Policy (Appendix C), fall in three buckets: **Non-Regulatory** (no recertification), **Regulatory** (must be certified *before* deployment), **Regulatory/Emergency Fix** (may deploy immediately but **must be submitted to an ITL within 5 business days**, with classification records retained for AGCO).
- **The change trigger that catches payment code:** a Technology Compliance Confirmation is required prior to implementation of substantial changes and annually, where "substantial" = a new gaming integration **or a change to a technology control used to meet a regulatory standard** (Notification Matrix S1B). Deposit-limit enforcement, geolocation checks, withdrawal name-matching, negative-balance guards, KYC gating and CSE integration are all such controls — changing them requires pre-implementation notification via iAGCO plus CEO and Chief Compliance Officer sign-off.
- Control Activity Matrix submitted prior to implementation of changes to control activities; must cover controls delivered via third-party suppliers including platform providers, and be independently audited (Notification Matrix S1A; 1.02 Req 2, 4, 5).
- Change-management standards proper: 5.13 SDLC, 5.15 test strategy, **5.16** (all changes documented/reviewed/tested/approved; Req 3 emergency changes; Req 4 dev/prod segregation of duties; Req 5 dedicated change accounts), **5.17 Req 1** ("There must be a mechanism to validate that installed software is the certified software"), 5.18–5.23, 5.52/5.53, 5.57/5.58 (promotion by production support, not developers), **5.60/5.61** (production access restricted from dev personnel; no development code in production), **5.62** (verify integrity of deployed software), 5.64/5.65 third-party code, 4.11 logical separation of prod/test/dev.
- Operators are responsible for contracted third parties, who must conduct themselves "as if they were bound by the same laws, regulations, and standards" — this reaches PSPs, wallet providers and platform vendors (1.19; also 1.18, 1.20).

### Deposits & permitted payment methods

- **Cryptocurrency is banned as a deposit method**: "Cryptocurrency is not legal tender and shall not be accepted" (note to **5.69**). Deposits must be "appropriately authorized by a financial services provider" (5.69 Req 1).
- **Credit cards are permitted, with no Ontario-specific restriction.** No standard restricts card funding, and the US operator moratoria were not extended to Ontario. AGCO's stated position (Jul 2026) is that its framework targets behavioural indicators rather than payment rails. A gateway that blocks cards in Ontario is over-restricting; one that treats a US card ban as globally applicable will mis-serve this market (HIGH that no prohibition exists; MEDIUM-HIGH on the AGCO commentary).
- **Operators shall not extend credit or lend money to players, refer players to credit providers, or imply that a player should seek additional credit** (**5.74**). Note this constrains the operator's conduct, not the card rails.
- **No negative balances** (**5.75**): account with a negative balance must be suspended with no transactions permitted until cleared, and "**no bet will be accepted that could result in a negative funds balance**". Resettlement of bets is permitted where reasonable and necessary (guidance to 5.75).
- **Funds shall not be transferred between player accounts** (**5.79**) — absolute, with none of the Registrar-approval carve-out that the legacy OLG standard had. Third parties may not access a player's account (3.12); one account per gaming site (3.09).
- All balances, bets and payouts in **Canadian dollars** (5.76 Req 1; 2.15.1 Req 7).
- Unambiguous fee information **before** a deposit or withdrawal (5.77); full disclosure of deposit/withdrawal rules, restrictions and access conditions (5.78).
- **Prepaid cards / e-money vouchers carry a hard numeric cap that is NOT in the public Standards.** Quoted from an iGO requirement numbered **"Regulation 8.1 — Deposit"**: *"a limit of $250 a week (on a rolling 7-calendar day basis) must be applied to Player deposits using prepaid cards/e-money vouchers. In addition, a limit of 5 prepaid cards/e-money vouchers can be used for deposit per week. This requirement applies to all prepaid cards/e-money vouchers regardless of the type (i.e., reloadable vs. non-reloadable)."* The same provision requires the operator to **work with its PSP to identify prepaid cards** and to **establish transaction monitoring rules for suspicious activity**, documented in its AML procedures, to mitigate prepaid-card risk.
  - **Provenance and health warning:** this text was recovered from an internal engineering requirements page at another Ontario operator, not from a regulator publication. It is **absent** from the Registrar's Standards for Internet Gaming (searched verbatim) and from AGCO's public "8. Funds Management (iGaming)" page (searched for "prepaid", "250", "e-money", "voucher" — zero hits), and no public source states it. Treat the substance as HIGH confidence that such a requirement exists and is enforced in production by at least one Ontario operator, but the instrument and section number as **MEDIUM/unverified** — obtain the iGO source document before citing "Regulation 8.1" in anything client-facing.
  - Practical consequence for the review: Ontario has **no** credit-card restriction but **does** have a prepaid cap. A gateway that treats "cards are unrestricted in Ontario" as the whole story will miss this, and the limit is per-instrument-type and per-count, not just per-amount — so it needs prepaid BIN identification, not just a spend counter.
- Otherwise no Ontario-specific rule found for Interac e-Transfer, e-wallets or eCash — permitted under the general 5.69/5.70 + AML frame. Interac e-Transfer is the dominant local rail (Interac Online Banking for deposits only; e-Transfer for both directions).

### Withdrawals & payout timelines

- **There is no numeric withdrawal SLA in Ontario.** The obligation is outcome-based: withdrawals permitted "in an accurate and complete fashion and **as soon as is practicable**, subject to appropriate authorization and verification" (**5.71**); payouts "within a reasonable time of winning" (4.16). The enforceable number is the operator's **own published service standard** (1.16 Req 1) — so review the code against what the operator publishes, not against a regulatory figure. This is a genuine contrast with MI (10 business days) and KS (5 calendar days); do not import a US clock.
- **Closed-loop verification before payout** (**5.70** Req 1): verify the withdrawal is made by a holder of the account **and** is transferred to an account of which the player is a legal holder. Interac e-Transfer to a non-matching email/name is the classic failure mode.
- Withholding funds during investigation is expressly contemplated in three places: **4.10** (gaming system fault — may include suspension of betting, withholding of funds, refund of bets; decisions must be fair, reasonable and in good faith); **4.33** (suspicious betting — may suspend/cancel betting or withhold associated customer funds, and the operator "**must ensure that it has reserved itself the authority**" to do so, i.e. the T&Cs must actually say it); **6.03 Req 2** (risk-based escalating measures including refusal of transactions).
- **Reverse withdrawals are not regulated in Ontario.** The words do not appear in the Standards and no bulletin on point was located. Do **not** assert an Ontario reverse-withdrawal restriction — it is a common and incorrect assumption. Whether the OA or RG Check criteria address it is an open question (LOW).
- Balance recovery on dormancy/deactivation (3.22, 3.17); balance return on self-exclusion (2.14 Req 10; 2.14.1 Req 11).

### Player funds segregation / reserve

- **The Standards impose no trust-account, no segregation formula and no reserve calculation on the operator.** They require only that "player funds shall be clearly and appropriately managed" (**5.72**) and then place the substantive duty on iGO: "**iGaming Ontario shall take steps to ensure that all player funds deposited in respect of igaming lottery schemes conducted and managed by iGaming Ontario are subject to oversight by iGaming Ontario and available to players**" (**5.73**). Materially unlike NJ/PA/MI reserve regimes — a reviewer expecting a reserve computation will find nothing to test, and that absence is not itself an operator defect.
- The operational mechanics sit in the non-public iGO OA (MEDIUM): iGO holds **performance security** from operators (cash, letter of credit, surety bond or other acceptable instrument) in a segregated account, drawable to satisfy operator debts, losses, "or for any winnings not paid by Gaming Operators"; and the **OA s. 10.01 Report on Key Internal Controls** is delivered via an Agreed-Upon Procedures engagement whose control objectives include "**segregation of GGR funds and controls over player wallets and unsettled wagers**". That AUP is the closest thing to a testable segregation obligation — ask the client for it.
- Operator-side: controls supporting financial reports compliant with applicable accounting standards (1.04). AGCO-side: audited financial statements on request (Notification Matrix S2C) and an annual Statement of Assurance (S6). iGO reporting calendar reportedly 30 Apr / 15 May / 15 Jun (MEDIUM — single advisory source; confirm against the client's OA and iGO Finance Policy).

### Responsible gambling

- **Limits are offered, not defaulted** (**2.23**): easy and obvious way to set financial and time-based limits at registration and any time after; players given the **option** to set loss and deposit limits during registration; operators must offer **deposit limits** and **loss limits** (amount lost = spend minus winnings); durations offered must include **24 hours, 7 days and one month**; "where the player sets simultaneous periods … **the lowest limit must apply**". **There is no mandatory default cap in Ontario.**
- **Asymmetric limit timing** (**2.24**): a request to **relax or eliminate** an existing limit may be implemented only after a cooling-off period of **at least 24 hours**, only on player request, and "**gaming limits must be enforced by the gaming system**" (Req 2). No waiting period is prescribed for a **decrease**, so it should apply immediately — that is an inference from the drafting, not an express statement (MEDIUM).
- **Breaks in play** (2.13): must offer one day, one week, one month, two month and three month breaks; no wagers during the break.
- **Operator self-exclusion** (**2.14** [amended May 2026]): terms must include six months, one year and five years; immediate logout and no login for the duration; stop all marketing/incentives/promotions as soon as practicable; **wager refunded if the player self-excludes before the event commences**; register of excluded persons; active steps to detect and remove breaching persons; mechanism to return unused funds on request.
- **Centralized Self-Exclusion ("BetGuard"), live 14 May 2026, administered by iGO** (**2.14.1**): participation mandatory. iGO adds registrants **within one hour**. Operators must prevent Centrally Self-Excluded Persons from creating new accounts *or* accessing existing ones; **within 24 hours** of registry addition stop all marketing/incentives/promotions; **within 24 hours** cancel and refund outstanding wagers to the player wallet, **except** where the person was added less than 24 hours before the event commences; log the person out immediately on confirmation; provide a mechanism to return unused funds on request or automatically; and **manage system disruptions affecting the ability to perform registry checks** — i.e. the fail-open/fail-closed behaviour is itself a regulated question. Self-excluded and centrally self-excluded persons remain **eligible for prizes** (3.01 Req 2) — their funds must not be voided.
- **Net position display** for slots: session must display net position (winnings minus losses since session start) in CAD (2.21); means to track passage of time (2.22). Account visibility must include deposit/withdrawal history, balance, **method and source of funds**, totals wagered/won/lost, and balance at session start and end (3.15).
- **Harm monitoring must ingest payment signals** (2.10): monitor risk profiles and behaviours including, per AGCO, spending patterns, time indicators and "the use of higher-risk or multiple payment methods".
- **Bonus and deposit-match advertising is heavily constrained** (2.05–2.07): advertising communicating inducements, bonuses or credits is **prohibited except on the operator's own gaming site and via direct marketing after active player consent** — the inducements themselves are not prohibited, the public advertising of them is. Material conditions disclosed at first presentation with the rest no more than one click away; must not be described as "**free**" if the player must risk or lose their own money or if conditions attach to their own money; must not be described as "**risk-free**" if the player must incur any loss or risk their own money to use it or withdraw winnings. No product or promotion "not reasonably attainable without incurring substantial losses" (2.04 Req 15); limit marketing to known high-risk players (2.03 Req 8).
- RG statement, link and **ConnexOntario** number on the registration page and within the player account; live 24/7 customer support (2.09; 2.11 Req 5). **RG Check accreditation** required by iGO within the first two years of the OA and maintained thereafter.

### KYC / age / identity

- **Minimum age 19** (3.01 Req 1(a)), except an 18-year-old accessing the site solely to purchase a lottery ticket. Not 18, not 21 — a US-derived 21 gate is over-restrictive and a US 18 gate is a breach.
- **KYC must complete before the account is created**, therefore before first deposit and first wager: player information "shall be **demonstrated to be complete, accurate and validated before a player account is created**" (**3.04**). Minimum set: name, date of birth, address, log-on identification method, contact information, **and information required by the PCMLTFA and its regulations**. Ontario has **no** deposit-before-verification grace window of the kind some US states allow — any "pending verification but can transact" state is a defect.
- Ineligible categories that must be blocked include self-exclusion participants, court-order restricted persons, persons excluded under GCA s. 3.6(1), operator officers/directors, certain supplier employees, AGCO members/employees, and OLG/iGO officers and employees (3.01 Req 1).
- **Re-verification on list change** (3.03): if the list of prohibited and excluded individuals changes, all registered player information shall be re-verified.
- One account per gaming site (3.09); authentication before account access and before gambling (3.08); **players must be offered MFA** (3.12 Req 1); no third-party account access (3.12); auditable trail of account creation/activation/deactivation/changes including identification and verification information (3.10).
- **KYC, geolocation and self-exclusion control failures are immediate-notification incidents** to AGCO via iAGCO (Notification Matrix I3).

### Geolocation / jurisdiction

- **Games shall be provided only within Ontario**, unless conducted jointly with another province's government (**3.02**).
- Mechanisms must **detect and dynamically monitor** player location and **block unverified attempts to play**; rechecks "**at reasonable intervals determined by the Operator**" that minimise the risk of play outside Ontario; plus detection of software, programs and **virtualization** capable of circumventing location detection (3.02 Req 1–2). **No prescribed interval, accuracy radius or approved vendor** — so any interval in the code must be defensible on its own analysis, and there is no AGCO number to test against.
- **Critical payments nuance: the geofence applies to PLAY, not to money movement.** Per iGO, "players can register and manage their account (e.g. deposit or withdraw funds) while physically outside of Ontario". A gateway that hard-blocks deposits or withdrawals on non-Ontario IP is **over-blocking** relative to the rule; one that allows wagering without a fresh location check is non-compliant. Both directions are findings.
- Go-live evidence requires a description of how 3.02 controls were **validated** for accuracy and effectiveness "across the majority of expected player device and network connection types".
- **Shared liquidity: permitted in principle, prohibited in practice today.** *Reference re iGaming Ontario*, 2025 ONCA 770 (12 Nov 2025) upheld the iGO model under Criminal Code s. 207(1)(a) 4–1, conditioned on Ontario retaining actual operational control and Ontario players accessing games through Ontario-dedicated sites; inter-provincial pooling needs a government-to-government agreement. The case is **under appeal to the Supreme Court of Canada** (undecided as at Aug 2026). The operative rule for a code review is iGO's: "**Any games involving liquidity pools outside of Ontario are prohibited at this time.**"

### AML overlays (provincial layer over the Canadian federal baseline)

- **6.02**: AML policies and procedures supporting PCMLTFA obligations shall be implemented and enforced; copies of all reports filed with FINTRAC and supporting records made available to the Registrar; **Req 2 — "Operators shall ensure their anti-money laundering internal controls align with those of the designated reporting entity under the PCMLTFA"** (i.e. iGO is likely the reporting entity; see the Canadian baseline for the unresolved operator-vs-iGO question).
- **6.03**: policies specifying, on a risk basis, when the operator will **ascertain and reasonably corroborate a player's source of funds**; risk-based escalating measures including **refusal of transactions** or exclusion of the player; lawful information sharing with other operators about high-risk or suspicious activity.
- **6.01 Req 3**: monitor player and employee transactions, including ongoing analysis of incident reports and suspicious transactions.
- Notification Matrix: **I2B** monthly to OPP (patrons excluded/limited following an ML risk assessment); **I2C** monthly (iGO notification of STRs filed with FINTRAC by operators and iGO); **S3** results of a FINTRAC audit the month following receipt.

### Records, reporting & data

- **Retention floor is three years** (**1.09**) — "unless otherwise stated". The PCMLTFA five-year requirement governs AML records, so retention config must be `max(provincial, federal)`. Do not let a 3-year Ontario setting become the global default.
- Records sufficient for Registrar investigations, dispute resolution and complaints, tracking all relevant player information **including funds information**, sessions, events and configuration changes; compensating manual controls if logging is interrupted; capability for **custom and on-demand reports to the Registrar** (4.02–4.04). Continuous independent monitoring of cash and cash-equivalent handling verifying prize payment to the proper person and accuracy of financial transactions, with continuous logs tracking financial accounting (4.01 Req 1–2).
- All player account transactions recorded and logged accurately, uniquely identifiable and traceable to one account (3.13, 3.16); account adjustments accurate, made only by authorized individuals, logged, with clear specific reasons given to the player (5.80–5.82).
- Audit rights: internal and external auditors granted access to all relevant systems and documentation; the **Registrar may direct the operator to retain an independent auditor who reports directly to the Registrar** (1.10 Req 2–3); control activities available to AGCO (1.02 Req 3); management overrides documented (1.03).
- Payments-relevant immediate notifications: **I1** any gaming system fault resulting in under- or overpayments to a player, and any data breach of sensitive data; **I2A** any incident of theft including unauthorized access to funds and **abuse of bonusing**; **I6** unplanned closure of the gaming site or the CSE registry; **I7** (monthly) inappropriate use of administrator accounts. Also S2A/S2B/S2D audit and committee reporting, S5 quarterly supplier list, S7 periodic regulatory data via the AGCO SFTP exchange.
- **The iGO reporting regime is concrete, and several of its definitions are code-bearing.** Spec is the **iGaming Ontario Data Dictionary** (client-held xlsx; iGO's own reference build is `iGo_Operator_Data_Dictionary_20230215`). Identifier conventions: **`iGO-DSnn`** = a data set/report, **`iGO-DEnnn`** = a data element.
  - **Four MVP reports, delivered by SFTP to an iGO-hosted FTP (operator supplies IP addresses for allow-listing), and files must land by 10:00 ET:** `iGO-DS02` Player Transaction Data (**daily**; the AML report; ~129 fields), `iGO-DS12` GGR Data Template (**weekly**), `iGO-DS14` Player Activity Data (**weekly**), `iGO-DS18` Gaming Activity Data (**weekly**). Filenames are templated on the iGO Operator ID and submission/period dates. Further AML reports (`DS05`, `DS08`, `DS21`, `DS23`) are delivered manually via iGO SharePoint; **`DS23` is the FINTRAC F2R Access Report** that provisions staff portal access.
  - **Time-boxing definitions that must match the code's aggregation windows:** **Gaming Day = 00:00:00–23:59:59 ET**; **Operating Week = Sunday 00:00:00 – Saturday 23:59:59 ET**. A UTC or US-timezone day boundary silently mis-buckets every daily and weekly report.
  - **Reports must be in CAD**, and must contain **real Canadian data** — US production data may not be transformed to stand in for it, including for pre-launch testing.
  - **Void and cancellation accounting is prescribed:** if the void/cancel occurs on the **same** Gaming Day as the original, the net reported value is zero; if on a **different** Gaming Day, it is reported **as a negative value on the day the void occurred**. A ledger that retro-adjusts the original day's figures will not reconcile.
  - **`WithdrawableWin` has a narrow regulatory meaning:** winnings from *Promotional Play* that have become freely withdrawable — i.e. bonus funds converted to cash once wagering requirements are met. **Cash winnings from cash wagers are excluded.** Bonus lifecycle reports separately as `PromoAdjustments` (award), `PromoPlayerWagers` (wagering with bonus funds) and `PromoPlayerWin` (win before conversion). Conflating promotional and cash winnings in one balance field makes DS14/DS18 unreportable.
  - **Third-party involvement must NOT be hard-coded.** iGO's explicit position: the prohibition is not determined by operator policy but **by the player's attestation at the time of the transaction**, and must be assessed per transaction rather than defaulted to "No" — so there must be a captured, per-transaction attestation value.
  - **Deposits ≥ CAD $3,000**: recording Purpose of Transaction as "For Gaming Purposes" is acceptable to iGO. Note this threshold aligns with the PCMLTFR s.74(2)(f) receipt-of-funds record trigger — see the Canadian baseline.
  - **The FINTRAC Location ID (`iGO-DE361`) is a mandatory field in every transaction record**, provisioned through onboarding after AGCO registration. Identity-verification method selection (government ID / credit file / dual process) follows the **iGO AML/ATF Job Aid**, "Methods to Verify Player Identity" — which determines which ID sub-fields are mandatory.
  - **Data elements worth checking exist at all**, because they are commonly absent in a US-first warehouse: `DE273` TotalGameplayDuration, `DE277` Fees, `DE147`/`DE149`/`DE191–372` "reasonable measures" fields (device ID, IP, employer/occupation), `DE513` BreakStatus, `DE520` SEStatus, `DE525` HighRisk, `DE310–315` and `DE528–529` wagers split by product and by mobile/PC. Also required and often unstored: full bank name, full sending-party account number, and the financial institution's full address.
  - Provenance: client launch documentation and iGO's own written answers, not a public AGCO publication (HIGH on the definitions quoted above, MEDIUM on exact DE ranges — get the Data Dictionary).
- **Privacy is a hard constraint on payment data reuse** (**5.26**): Req 1 — data collection and protection for player personal information shall meet the **Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act**; **Req 2 — "Player personal information shall only be used for the lottery schemes conducted and managed respectively by the OLG or iGaming Ontario, unless there is prior approval."** That is a direct bar on secondary use of transaction data for analytics, cross-product marketing, vendor reuse or model training without approval. Plus 5.24 data governance, 5.25 sensitive-data protection and off-site backups, 5.27 integrity in transit, 5.36/5.37 key management. Operators act as agents of iGO, which pulls player data into a FIPPA-facing framework with IPC oversight and FOI exposure layered over PIPEDA (MEDIUM on that characterisation). No data-residency prohibition — offshore processing is contemplated subject to notice and contractual controls.

## Code review focus

- **Prepaid-card cap enforced server-side, by count and by amount, on a rolling 7-day window**: CAD $250 per rolling 7 calendar days AND a maximum of 5 distinct prepaid cards/e-money vouchers per week, applying to reloadable and non-reloadable alike. Check that (a) prepaid instruments are actually *identified* (PSP/BIN classification, not self-declaration — note that at least one operator defaults unknown-provider cards to "treat as prepaid, each a different card", which is the conservative choice), (b) the window is rolling rather than calendar-week, (c) the card-count limit exists at all — it is the one most likely to be missing, (d) the deposit is rejected rather than flagged, and (e) breaches are visible to ops/AML tooling, since the same provision requires transaction-monitoring rules for prepaid risk.
- **Crypto funding must be blocked server-side** for the Ontario tenant (5.69 note), not merely hidden in the UI. Check for any crypto PSP, stablecoin rail or crypto-backed BIN reachable from an ON build.
- **Withdrawal destination ownership matching** (5.70 Req 1): confirm the payout instrument is legally held by the account holder, not merely "previously used to deposit". Hunt payouts to a mismatched name, Interac e-Transfer to a non-matching address, and admin override paths that skip the check without a logged reason.
- **No player-to-player transfer capability at all** (5.79): grep internal balance transfer, gifting, "send to friend", poker chip transfer, shared/family wallets — and confirm disablement at the service layer for ON, since Ontario has no approval carve-out.
- **Negative balance prevented pre-authorisation, not corrected after** (5.75): does the bet-acceptance path reject any wager that *could* go negative, including cash-out, bonus unwind, chargeback reversal and rounding? On occurrence, is the account suspended and all transactions blocked until cleared? Check concurrency between bet and withdraw paths.
- **Limit engine semantics** (2.23, 2.24 Req 2): 24h/7d/1-month windows supported; **lowest limit wins when simultaneous**; loss limit computed as spend minus winnings, not spend; enforced server-side at both deposit and wager; bonus funds and reversals treated consistently in the limit ledger.
- **Asymmetric limit-change path** (2.24): look for one `updateLimit()` treating increases and decreases alike. Increases/removals must be queued ≥24h against a recorded player request; decreases immediate. Check CRM, VIP tooling and bulk migration scripts for paths that relax a limit with no player request.
- **CSE / BetGuard integration with hard timers** (2.14.1): registry check at registration, at login and recurrently; blocks both account creation and access; ≤24h marketing suppression; ≤24h cancel-and-refund of outstanding wagers to wallet with the <24h-before-event carve-out; immediate forced logout; unused-funds return. **Most important: what happens when the registry is unavailable — fail closed or fail open?**
- **KYC gating before account creation** (3.04): no path where a deposit is accepted or funds held in a pending-verification state; DOB gate enforces **19**; PCMLTFA-required fields captured pre-creation.
- **Geolocation on wagering, not on money movement** (3.02 + iGO FAQ): check both failure directions — wagering without a fresh Ontario check or without VPN/virtualization detection, *and* deposits/withdrawals/registration blocked for players legitimately outside Ontario. Confirm the recheck interval is configurable and its value documented.
- **Withholding states are first-class and disclosed** (5.71, 4.10, 4.33, 1.16): a "held for review" withdrawal state with reason code, owner, SLA timer and player-facing messaging — not withdrawals sitting in `PENDING` forever. Confirm T&Cs actually reserve the authority 4.33 requires, and that published service standards match the code's timers.
- **Gaming Day / Operating Week boundaries** (ET, not UTC, not US-local): grep `date_trunc`, `startOfDay`, `TZ`, `America/`, `UTC`, `gamingDay`, `operatingWeek`. Daily and weekly regulatory aggregations must bucket on Eastern Time with a Sunday week start, and the same boundary must be used by the reporting job and the ledger.
- **Void/cancel accounting across day boundaries**: does a cancellation on a later Gaming Day post a negative entry on the cancellation day, or retro-adjust the original day? Only the former reconciles to iGO's rule.
- **Promotional vs cash winnings separation**: grep `withdrawableWin`, `promoBalance`, `bonusConverted`, `winnings`. `WithdrawableWin` must capture only promotional funds converted to withdrawable cash, with cash-wager winnings excluded, and the promo lifecycle must be separable into award / wagered / pre-conversion win.
- **Per-transaction third-party attestation**: grep `thirdParty`, `onBehalfOf`, `isThirdParty`. A hardcoded `false`/`"No"` is a defect — iGO requires a per-transaction player attestation value.
- **Financial audit trail** (3.13, 3.16, 4.01 Req 2, 5.80–5.82, 1.09): every deposit, withdrawal, adjustment, bonus grant/forfeit, reversal and resettlement logged with unique ID, one-account traceability, named actor and player-facing reason for adjustments. Look for hard-delete or in-place UPDATE on ledger rows. Retention ≥3 years provincially, ≥5 for AML. Confirm `method and source of funds` is persisted and exposed (3.15).
- **CAD-only display and pre-transaction fee disclosure** in the cashier (5.76 Req 1, 2.15.1 Req 7, 5.77, 5.78).
- **Secondary-use fence on payment data** (5.26 Req 2): any analytics, marketing, vendor or model-training pipeline consuming transaction data without documented approval is a finding.
- **Change-management evidence for payment/RG/geo controls** (Notification Matrix S1B; 5.16, 5.17 Req 1, 5.58, 5.60–5.62; ITL 5-business-day emergency rule): for commits touching limits, self-exclusion blocking, geolocation, KYC gating, negative-balance guards or withdrawal verification — is there a substantial-change determination, reviewer separate from author, no developer production write access, a certified-build validation mechanism, and no development code in the production artifact?
