# Nevada — Nevada Gaming Control Board / Nevada Gaming Commission (NGCB/NGC)

**Code:** NV · **Products:** sportsbook (mobile + retail); online poker only for online casino-type play (no other online casino) · **Key authority:** NRS 463 (Licensing and Control of Gaming, incl. NRS 463.350 age 21, NRS 463.016425 "interactive gaming" defined); NGC Regulation 22 (Race Books & Sports Pools, Rev. 11/24); Regulation 5 (Operation of Gaming Establishments, Rev. 12/24, incl. 5.225 Wagering Accounts, new 5.047 funding restrictions, 5.115 periodic-payment Reserve, 5.170 self-limitation); Regulation 5A (Interactive Gaming); Regulation 6 (Accounting/Internal Controls, incl. 6.090, 6.150(5)); **Regulation 6A (Cash Transactions Prohibitions, Reporting, and Recordkeeping) — REPEALED in full 6/30/2007**, superseded by federal BSA/Title 31 filing directly with FinCEN

## Payments-relevant requirements

### Licensing & change management

- Online poker (the only "interactive gaming" product) requires a separate operator/service-provider license under NRS 463.016425 / Regulation 5A; historically only two operators have been licensed (Real Gaming/South Point Poker LLC; WSOP/Caesars Interactive Entertainment) — confidence MEDIUM on the current count, verify against the NGC's live online-gaming licensee list. ([gaming.nv.gov/gaming-commission/online-gaming](https://www.gaming.nv.gov/gaming-commission/online-gaming/))
- Mobile/online sports wagering is NOT "interactive gaming" under the NRS 463.016425 exclusion for race books/sports pools using Board-approved "communications technology" — it is licensed and regulated as an extension of a retail race book/sports pool under Regulation 22, not under Regulation 5A. (MEDIUM — exclusion referenced in secondary sources; verify exact statutory carve-out language against NRS 463.016425 primary text.)
- **April 2026 NGC amendments to Regulations 5 and 25** (adopted at the Commission's April 2026 meeting, following a February 2026 NGCB recommendation): the person responsible for a licensee's overall compliance must be designated a **key employee** (subject to Commission licensing/suitability); individuals who primarily oversee the AML program must be registered as **gaming employees**; licensees must report to the Board within **10 business days** when a gaming employee is terminated for intentional/willful or repeated-after-notice AML-policy violations. (HIGH — corroborated by Grant Thornton, Womble Bond Dickinson, and Lexology/National Law Review write-ups of the same NGC action.)
- Same April 2026 package adds **new Regulation 5.047** restricting business-entity funding of patron wagering (see Deposits below); adopted with a **6-month implementation window**, so the funding-restriction provision does not bind until roughly **late October 2026**. (HIGH — Grant Thornton + bettorsinsider both independently report the ~October 2026 effective date.)
- Regulation 5.225 (wagering accounts) was itself amended by the NGC on **12/19/2024** to let non-restricted licensees allow wagering-account funds to pay for non-gaming purchases (bar tabs, retail) at the licensee or an affiliated/tenant business within Nevada; NGC requested an annual report on expansion requests starting December 2025. (HIGH — iGamingBusiness, corroborated by operator T&Cs.)

### Deposits & permitted payment methods

- Regulation 22 does not itself enumerate sports-wagering-account funding methods; Reg 22.160 cross-references Regulation 5.225 for account-funding mechanics rather than specifying methods independently. (MEDIUM — based on partial-text extraction of Reg 22; verify full 22.160 text.)
- Regulation 5.225 leaves the specific funding channels largely to the licensee's approved internal controls (cash/cage, ACH, debit/credit card, etc.); real-world operator terms (e.g., BetMGM Nevada) describe deposits "by any method allowed under Regulation 5.225" subject to the operator's own T&Cs. (MEDIUM — regulation text itself not fully retrieved; corroborated only via operator-facing secondary sources.)
- Regulation 5.225 includes an anti-layering control: procedures must ensure funds deposited into or withdrawn from a wagering account are not transferred to a *different* account unless that other account is verified as controlled by the same patron. (MEDIUM — sourced via an operator T&C page paraphrasing the regulation, not the primary PDF text; verify exact subsection.)
- **New Regulation 5.047 (adopted 4/2026, binding ~10/2026):** licensees must take reasonable steps to ensure no **business entity** directly funds a patron's wagering activity — via deposit into a wagering account, front money/safekeeping deposit, or casino credit extended on the business entity's behalf. Carved-out exceptions: sole proprietorships, other Nevada-licensed casinos, financial institutions where the licensee can confirm the funds originated from the patron's own personal account, and charitable-gaming events. (HIGH — corroborated by Grant Thornton, Womble Bond Dickinson, Lexology/National Law Review, and bettorsinsider, all describing the same rule.)

### Withdrawals & payout timelines

- Regulation 22.145(6) (as extracted): winning-wager payouts must be posted "as soon as reasonably practicable after the event is declared official" — there is **no fixed numeric payout SLA** analogous to Colorado's 24-hour rule. (MEDIUM — partial PDF-text extraction; verify exact subsection and current rev.)
- No statewide numeric account-withdrawal-completion deadline (e.g., a "within N days" rule like Maryland's 7-day requirement) was located in Regulation 5 or 22 during this pass. **Confidence LOW — verify directly with NGCB/compliance counsel** before assuming NV has (or lacks) a hard withdrawal SLA.
- Illustrative (non-primary) operator T&C language describes withdrawal rails as: in person at a registration location, transfer to a verified non-transferable reloadable prepaid card, or "any other method allowed by applicable law and agreed to" — implying flexibility rather than a mandated single rail.

### Player funds segregation / reserve

- **Regulation 22.040(1)(a):** each race book/sports pool must at all times maintain a reserve of not less than the **greater of $25,000** or the sum of (i) patron wagering-account balances, (ii) pending/undetermined wager liability, and (iii) unpaid winning wagers. (HIGH — direct PDF-text extraction of Reg 22, Rev. 11/24; verify exact subsection lettering against the current published revision.)
- **Regulation 6.150(5):** licensees generally must maintain cash or cash equivalents sufficient to reasonably protect patrons against default; falling below required minimums is itself an unsuitable method of operation. (HIGH.)
- **Regulation 5.115** defines a separate, restricted "Reserve" account used exclusively to fund **periodic payments** of prizes — relevant only where winnings are paid out via annuity/trust rather than lump sum (periodic payments barred for payouts of $100,000 or less). This is distinct from the day-to-day Reg 22.040 sports-pool reserve. (HIGH on the definition; MEDIUM on full applicability to a given operator's product mix.)

### Responsible gambling

- **Regulation 5.170:** wherever a licensee offers credit, check cashing, or direct marketing, it must offer a **self-limitation program** letting a patron voluntarily restrict access to those specific services (not a full account freeze); removal from marketing lists must occur within **10 days** of the patron's request; problem-gambling signage/helpline info required near gaming areas, cage areas, and ATMs in gaming areas; front-line staff must be trained on problem-gambling symptoms and referral (without being required to diagnose). (HIGH.)
- A statewide, list-based self-exclusion program is understood to exist but its specific current citation was **not independently verified** in this pass. Confidence LOW — verify exact regulation number with compliance/legal before building against it.
- **SB 362 (2025 session, BDR 41-1026)** would have codified/expanded the Reg 5.170 self-limitation program to explicitly cover mail, telephone, text-message, and email marketing channels — it **died in committee** (no further action permitted under Joint Standing Rule 14.3.1, effective 4/12/2025) and was **not enacted**. Do not build against it; treat as a failed/pending proposal only. (HIGH on "died" status per LegiScan/TrackBill legislative tracking. Note: an earlier AI-generated web-search synthesis encountered during this research also claimed SB 362 "expanded the definition of interactive gaming to include sports pools" — that claim could not be corroborated against any primary legislative source and is very likely a search-summarization error; it is explicitly excluded here and should not be relied upon.)

### KYC / age / geolocation

- **NRS 463.350:** persons under 21 may not play, be allowed to play, place wagers at, or collect winnings from any gambling game, slot machine, race book, sports pool, or pari-mutuel operator; violation is a misdemeanor. (HIGH — verified against Justia's 2025 NRS text.)
- **Regulation 22.140(1):** wagers are restricted to patrons physically within Nevada, or from other states/foreign jurisdictions where the wager is legal under applicable federal and other law (interstate/international pooling). (HIGH — direct extraction; verify full subsection.)
- **In-person registration wrinkle (payments/KYC-relevant):** **Regulation 22.140(6)(a)(1)** requires a patron to personally appear before an employee of the licensee, or present identification before an employee at the premises of the book, to establish a mobile/online sports wagering account. Nevada remains the only major sports-betting state still requiring physical presence for mobile-account signup; industry (notably larger multi-state operators) has pushed to remove it, but independent/single-state operators have opposed removal to avoid ceding advantage to multi-state brands, and no repeal has succeeded as of 2026. (HIGH — Reg 22 text + corroborated by The Nevada Independent's reporting.) Once registered in person, the account may be used remotely anywhere in Nevada subject to the Reg 22.140(1) geolocation gate.

### AML overlays

- **Regulation 6A ("Cash Transactions Prohibitions, Reporting, and Recordkeeping") was repealed in its entirety — sections 6A.010 through 6A.110 — effective close of business 6/30/2007.** Nevada ceded state-level CTR/SAR collection to FinCEN/federal BSA at that time; casinos file Currency Transaction Reports (Form 8300/BSA equivalent) and Suspicious Activity Reports directly with FinCEN under Title 31/BSA rather than through a standalone state instrument, and — per contemporaneous reporting — state regulators lost direct visibility into SARs/CTRs filed by Nevada casinos as a result. **This means Regulation 6A is not a currently-active state AML regime; treat any reference to "Reg 6A cash/AML requirements" as historical, and flag this explicitly to compliance/legal since it contradicts the common assumption that Reg 6A is still in force.** (HIGH — corroborated by the NGCB's own archived Reg 6A PDF, marked repealed section-by-section as of 6/30/07, plus the Nevada State Bar's "Goodbye 6A, Hello BSA" article and The Nevada Independent's reporting on the aftermath.)
- Regulation 22 retains some cash-transaction-adjacent language (a ~$10,000 individual-transaction identification/reporting reference and a ~$5,000-aggregate/30-day suspicious-transaction reporting reference were located in partial extraction as Reg 22.061 and 22.121) — these likely track federal BSA/Title 31 thresholds rather than constituting an independent state-level reporting channel, consistent with Reg 6A's 2007 repeal. Confidence LOW-MEDIUM on exact current subsection numbers and on whether these create any state-specific (vs. purely federal) reporting obligation — verify against the current Reg 22 PDF and with compliance/legal.
- The **live, current NV-specific AML overlay is the April 2026 Regulation 5/25 package** described under Licensing above: mandatory key-employee licensing for the compliance lead, gaming-employee registration for the AML-program owner, and a 10-business-day Board-notification requirement on AML-related terminations. (HIGH.)

### Records, reporting & data

- **Regulation 6.060:** records must be retained within Nevada for at least **5 years**; failure to produce records on request is itself an unsuitable method of operation. (HIGH.)
- **Regulation 6.120:** where casino credit is extended, licensees must document patron identification, credit-history verification, and collection-attempt logs at least every 90 days — this now intersects with the new Reg 5.047 business-entity-funding ban, since credit extended on behalf of a business entity is one of the categories the 2026 amendment restricts. (HIGH.)
- **Regulation 6.090:** licensees' written internal-control systems require annual written confirmation from an independent accountant that the system complies with Reg 6.090. (HIGH.)

## Code review focus

- Reserve snapshot: expose a live NV sports-pool reserve calculation (patron wagering-account balances + pending/undetermined wager liability + unpaid winning wagers) checked against the Reg 22.040 floor of `max($25,000, sum)`; keep this distinct from any Reg 5.115 periodic-payment "Reserve" used only for large (>$100,000) annuitized prize payouts.
- **Business-entity funding gate (Reg 5.047, binding ~Oct 2026):** before authorizing an NV wagering-account deposit, verify the funding source is not a business entity unless it falls into an allowed exception (sole proprietorship, another NV-licensed casino, a financial institution with confirmed personal-account origin, or a charitable-gaming event). This is a build-before-effective-date gap analogous in shape to Colorado's SB 26-131 credit-card date-gate — track the ~October 2026 deadline explicitly.
- Anti-layering control: block or explicitly verify any withdrawal whose destination account differs from the original deposit source unless the destination is confirmed to be controlled by the same patron (Reg 5.225) — relevant to any wallet-to-wallet transfer, gift, or third-party payout path.
- In-person registration gate: the NV onboarding/KYC flow must record and enforce an in-person verification event (Reg 22.140(6)(a)(1)) before a mobile wagering account goes live — flag any code path that treats NV like a remote-KYC state (e.g., reusing a generic multi-state onboarding flow without an NV-specific branch).
- Compliance-role tagging: if the codebase models compliance/approval roles or org data, confirm the designated AML-program owner and overall compliance lead are tagged as licensed gaming employee / key employee per the 2026 Reg 5/25 amendments, and that an AML-cause termination triggers a Board-notification workflow within the 10-business-day SLA.
- Payout timing: do not hardcode a fixed numeric settlement/withdrawal SLA for NV (no CO-style 24h or MD-style 7-day rule was confirmed) — any automated compliance alert keyed to a specific NV deadline should be treated as unverified until confirmed with compliance/legal.
- Non-gaming wagering-account spend: if the wallet/ledger FundType model assumes wagering-account balances are gaming-only, confirm it can accommodate NV's Reg 5.225 (eff. 12/19/2024) allowance for non-restricted licensees to let patrons spend those funds on non-gaming, in-venue purchases.
- Self-limitation vs. self-exclusion: confirm Reg 5.170's narrower "self-limit access to credit/check-cashing/direct-marketing" is modeled as distinct from any full-account self-exclusion state, and that marketing-list removal completes within 10 days of a patron request.
- Do not treat Regulation 6A as a live control in any AML rule engine or documentation — it has been repealed since 2007; NV's current AML surface is federal BSA/FinCEN filing plus the 2026 Reg 5/25 compliance-role amendments.
