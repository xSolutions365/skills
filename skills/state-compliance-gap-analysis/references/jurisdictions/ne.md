# Nebraska — Nebraska Racing and Gaming Commission (NRGC)

**Code:** NE · **Products:** retail-only sportsbook (in-person/kiosk at licensed racetrack enclosures) — **statewide mobile/online sports wagering is NOT legal as of 2026** · **Key authority:** Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 9-1101 to 9-1118 (Nebraska Racetrack Gaming Act) and §§ 9-1201 to 9-1209 (gaming tax); Title 296 NAC — Rules for Nebraska Racetrack Gaming Act (NRGC)

## ⚠️ Legal-status gate (read first)

Nebraska sports wagering was authorized by the 2020 ballot measures + the Nebraska Racetrack Gaming Act and launched retail-only in June 2023 at licensed racetrack enclosures (e.g., WarHorse Casino Lincoln). **All sports wagers must be placed in person or at a wagering kiosk within a Commission-approved designated sports wagering area inside a licensed racetrack enclosure (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 9-1110(1); Title 296 § 13.002.07).** There is no statutory or regulatory basis for remote/mobile/online account funding or wager placement anywhere in the Act or Title 296 — the entire framework is premised on physical presence at the racetrack enclosure.

Legislative attempts to authorize statewide mobile/internet sports wagering (LR3CA in 2024, LR20CA in the 2025 session) were each indefinitely postponed (LR20CA postponed April 2026) and did not pass. **Two related ballot petitions to amend Article III, § 24 of the Nebraska Constitution to permit internet/mobile sports wagering tied to licensed racetracks qualified for the November 3, 2026 general election ballot** — as of this writing (Aug 2026) that vote has **not yet occurred**, so mobile remains unauthorized. (HIGH confidence retail-only status; MEDIUM on exact petition mechanics/ballot language — verify final certified ballot text with compliance/legal before Nov 2026.)

**Implication for the operator:** if any the operator payment code path treats NE as deposit/wager-enabled by default (the common "rails on everywhere unless blocked" posture), that is an illegal-market exposure for NE specifically — remote deposits and remote sports wagers are not legal there today, independent of general UIGEA/Wire Act risk. See Code review focus below.

## Payments-relevant requirements

### Licensing & change management

- Authorized Gaming Operator license required to operate any game of chance (incl. sports wagering) within a licensed racetrack enclosure; one license per enclosure; $5M one-time license fee (payable over 5 years) + $50,000 annual review fee (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 9-1104, § 9-1106(6),(8)).
- Internal Control System (ICS) covering all MICS subject areas must be submitted to and approved by the Executive Director before gaming begins (Title 296 §§ 4.001–4.002).
- ICS amendments: submitted to the Executive Director; if the Commission does not approve/deny/respond within 30 days, the operator may implement the amendment as submitted (Commission retains authority to later require further amendment) (§ 4.003.01–.03). Emergency ICS amendments may be made immediately but the Executive Director must be notified before the change and the operator must submit the emergency amendment in writing within 24 hours (§ 4.003.05–.06).
- Annual independent audit of the ICS is required; findings/corrective actions documented and reported to management (§ 4.006).
- All gaming equipment/software (including sports wagering systems) must be tested and certified by a Commission-approved Independent Testing Laboratory before use, and sports wagering equipment/systems must be recertified annually (§ 5.001; § 13.005.01–.02).
- Sports Wagering Services Provider (a vendor operating/maintaining sports wagering software or hardware, or providing data aggregation/risk management/odds-setting services) must itself be licensed as a Gaming-Related Vendor; if an operator does not use one, the operator is treated as both operator and services provider (§ 13.0001.02; Ch.1 defs).

### Deposits & permitted payment methods

- **Credit cards are statutorily prohibited outright** — not date-gated, not jurisdiction-specific like some states: "Credit cards shall not be accepted by any authorized gaming operator for payment for any wager or to purchase coins, tokens, or other forms of credit to be wagered on any game of chance." An account for participating in a game of chance **may only be funded with cash, coins, a debit card, or a direct link to an account with a financial institution in the name of the player** (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 9-1109, HIGH). The Commission requires operators/applicants to demonstrate in the license/ICS application the ability to restrict credit-card transactions (§ 9-1109).
- Sports bets specifically may be funded/placed using: cash or vouchers, cash equivalent, bonus or promotional credit, gaming chips, funds within a patron account, debit cards, or any other Commission-approved means (Title 296 § 13.002.08A–G).
- **No credit or advance of value in any form** may be extended by an operator to enable play; failure to deposit a negotiable instrument for collection by the next banking day after receipt is itself deemed an extension of credit (§ 7.006.01). Vouchers may not be issued as a means of extending credit (§ 7.006.04).
- Check-cashing at the cashier's cage is limited to: personal checks, cashier's checks, money orders, traveler's checks, and wire transfer service checks — subject to operator-set, ICS-documented check-cashing privileges/limits (§ 7.006.02–.03).

### Withdrawals & payout timelines

- No explicit statewide numeric payout/withdrawal SLA (e.g., "within N days") was located in the Act or Title 296 (unlike, e.g., Maryland's 7-day rule or Colorado's 24-hour demand rule) — confidence LOW; verify against the operator's Commission-approved ICS/MICS, which is where such a timeline would actually live for NE, and against any NRGC bulletin/interpretation.
- Patron payment disputes: if an operator refuses to pay alleged winnings and cannot resolve the dispute with the patron within 48 hours, the operator must notify the Gaming Agent in writing with dispute details (§ 11.001.01). Patron must submit any dispute within 5 business days of the incident (§ 11.001.02); operator must investigate and respond to the patron within 14 calendar days of the complaint (§ 11.001.06); unresolved patron-account/wager-settlement disputes must be promptly escalated to the Commission (§ 11.001.09).
- Winning sports wagers/vouchers are honored through a period the operator itself establishes in its ICS/wagering rules (13.006.03D references "the period established by the Authorized Gaming Operator for honoring vouchers and winning sports wagers") — i.e., the payout window is operator-defined and Commission-approved, not a fixed statutory number. ### Player funds segregation / reserve

- **General cash-reserve rule (casino-wide, § 7.010):** operator must maintain a cash/cash-equivalent reserve sufficient to promptly pay winners and protect patrons against default. Formula: month 1 of operation ≥ 100% of *projected* weekly payout (avg daily payout × 7); month 2+ ≥ 100% of *actual* prior-month weekly payout (avg daily payout × 7). Cash equivalents = highly liquid investments with original maturity ≤ 3 months.
- **Sports-wagering-specific reserve (§ 13.006, separate and additional to § 7.010):** operator must submit a Commission-approved plan to maintain a reserve securing patron-account funds against default. Allowable forms: cash/cash-equivalents in a **Nebraska bank account segregated from operational funds**; irrevocable letter of credit from a Nebraska (or NE-chartered) bank; a bond; payment-processor reserves/receivables; any other Commission-approved form; or a combination. Minimum amount = greater of **$25,000** or the sum of: (a) daily ending cashable balance of all patron accounts, (b) pending patron-account withdrawals, (c) amounts accepted on sports wagers with undetermined outcomes, and (d) amounts owed but unpaid on vouchers/winning sports wagers. Funds in patron accounts must not be automatically transferred by the operator, and an operator may not require a patron to transfer funds to circumvent this rule (§ 13.006.04). Operator must file a monthly attestation of compliance and must have access to all patron account/transaction data to verify reserve sufficiency (§ 13.006.06); Commission may audit and order the reserve form/amount increased at any time (§ 13.006.07).

### Responsible gambling

- Compulsive Gambling Assistance Plan is a condition of initial licensure and of annual renewal; must include a plan manager, mission statement, intervention procedures, printed patron education materials, comprehensive employee training (with periodic refresher training), and minor-gambling prevention procedures (Title 296 §§ 12.001–12.002). Annual report on compulsive-gambling activities required (§ 12.003).
- Self-Exclusion Program (Ch. 14 / Neb. Rev. Stat. § 9-1118): individual selects a minimum duration — 1 year, 3 years, 5 years, or lifetime (lifetime only after ≥6 months on an active list) (§ 14.001.15). While excluded, the person may not collect winnings or recover losses from gambling activity — wagers are void from inception (§ 14.001.27; § 9-1118(6)) — and forfeits loyalty/reward points (§ 14.001.14G). Operators must refuse deposits/wagers/complimentary check-cashing/club programs to self-excluded persons and must not market to them (§ 14.001.28; § 9-1118(4)). Removal requires an in-person "exit session" with a designated agent (§ 14.001.18–.20).
- Involuntary Exclusion List (Ch. 8) is separate from self-exclusion; operators must exclude listed individuals and report attempted entry/play within 48 hours (§ 8.005.02–.03).
- No explicit patron-settable deposit/spend/time-limit mandate or cooling-off-period rule was located in the Act or Title 296 (confidence LOW — unlike CO/MD, NE's RG framework centers on the compulsive-gambling plan + self-exclusion rather than a mandated in-account limits API; verify against the operator's Commission-approved plan, since the plan's minimum-standards checklist leaves room for operator-designed limit tools under § 12.001.03A(xii)).

### KYC / age / geolocation

- Minimum age 21 for all games of chance including sports wagering; no person under 21 may play, participate, or be permitted to play (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 9-1104(4)–(5); § 9-1115 criminalizes both sides of underage participation). The designated sports wagering area must be inaccessible to under-21 patrons and must post a restricting sign (Title 296 § 9-1110(2)).
- Because wagering is retail/in-person only, "geolocation" in the CO/MD/NJ mobile-market sense (continuous device-location verification for remote wagers) has no NE analog today — the control is physical presence within the Commission-approved designated sports wagering area of the licensed racetrack enclosure (§ 9-1110(1)–(2)). No statewide account-funding identity-verification standard (e.g., government ID + database cross-check, MFA) equivalent to Maryland's COMAR 36.10.18.05(C) was located in the Act or Title 296 — confidence LOW; identity verification for check-cashing/credit-card-restriction purposes is delegated to each operator's Commission-approved ICS.
- Fingerprinting and FBI criminal-history checks are required for gaming-operator-license applicants, not patrons (§ 9-1108).

### AML overlays

- Title 296 requires operators to keep "records required to fully comply with all the federal financial record-keeping requirements as enumerated in 31 CFR 103" (the BSA/FinCEN recordkeeping regulations) as part of standard accounting records (§ 7.002.01C(xi)) — this is a state-rule cross-reference *into* federal BSA, not an independent state AML regime.
- No distinct Nebraska-specific AML program, CTR-equivalent, or suspicious-activity-reporting obligation beyond the federal BSA cross-reference was located in the Act or Title 296 (confidence LOW/MEDIUM — verify with compliance whether NRGC has issued separate AML guidance/bulletins outside Title 296).

### Records, reporting & data

- General records retention: 7 years minimum for correspondence with regulators, records concerning facility acquisition/construction/maintenance, and personnel files (§ 2.001.01); promotional/advertising/complimentary-distribution records ≥ 1 year (§ 2.001.02).
- Financial/accounting records retained per the operator's Commission-approved retention schedule; detailed subsidiary records must include patron-check activity (accepted, deposited, returned, written off), loans/advances/receivables, and BSA-cross-referenced records (§ 7.002.01C).
- Monthly, quarterly, and annual financial/statistical reports required; annual independent-CPA audit of financial statements + ICS due 3 months after fiscal year end (§§ 7.003–7.004).
- Gaming tax reported and paid via ACH-Debit by the 15th of each month, itemized weekly (Monday–Sunday), reporting form due to the Commission by noon Wednesday following week's end, funds due by 11 a.m. the following Thursday (§ 7.009.02–.06; Neb. Rev. Stat. § 9-1205).
- Patron complaint/dispute records retained ≥ 5 years and made available to the Commission on request (§ 11.001.04).
- Sports-wagering reserve monthly attestations retained and subject to Commission audit at any time (§ 13.006.06–.07).

## Code review focus

- **Jurisdiction/product gate for NE:** because statewide mobile/online sports wagering is not legal, any payment code path that treats NE like an "always-on" state (deposit + remote wager flows enabled by default, geofenced only for a hard "prohibited state" blocklist) is a compliance gap — NE needs to default to **retail-only / no-remote-deposit-or-wager** unless and until the Nov 3, 2026 ballot measure passes AND enabling legislation/regulation follows. Verify the geo/jurisdiction config explicitly marks NE as retail-only rather than silently falling through to a generic "legal state" bucket — illegal-market wagering carries UIGEA/Wire Act exposure independent of NE's own statute.
- **Absolute credit-card ban, no date-gating needed:** unlike CO's SB 26-131 (effective 8/12/2026), NE's credit-card prohibition (§ 9-1109) has applied since inception — confirm the payment method allowlist for NE never includes credit-card BINs or credit-funded prepaid/e-payment routes, and that the "restrict credit card transactions" capability required by § 9-1109 is demonstrable (BIN-range or card-type detection) rather than assumed.
- **No-credit-extension rule creates a same-banking-day deposit-instrument rule:** § 7.006.01 deems a negotiable instrument not deposited for collection by the next banking day an unlawful extension of credit — if any the operator flow issues markers/credit against an uncleared check or ACH item for NE, that needs same-day-next-banking-day settlement tracking, not float.
- **Dual reserve tracking, not just one:** NE requires *both* the general § 7.010 cash-reserve calc (trailing weekly avg payout × 7) *and* the § 13.006 sports-wagering-specific reserve (daily patron cashable balances + pending withdrawals + undetermined-outcome wager liability + unpaid vouchers/winnings, floor $25,000, in a segregated NE bank account or equivalent) — confirm the liability/reserve computation used for NE isn't reusing a single generic multi-state reserve number that conflates the two.
- **No patron-initiated fund sweep:** § 13.006.04 bars auto-transfer of patron-account funds and bars requiring a patron to transfer funds to dodge the reserve rule — audit any "sweep idle balances" or "auto-convert to bonus wallet" job for NE-specific exclusion.
- **Self-exclusion voids wagers, not just blocks new ones:** § 14.001.27/§ 9-1118(6) treat any wager placed by a self-excluded patron as void from inception with forfeited winnings — confirm the NE self-exclusion check blocks *and* nullifies rather than merely declining future deposits, and separately confirm loyalty/reward-point forfeiture logic exists for self-excluded NE accounts.
- **Payout SLA is operator/ICS-defined, not statutory** — do not hardcode a NE payout-timeline constant sourced from another state's rule (e.g., MD's 7 days or CO's 24 hours); the actual NE number lives in the licensee's Commission-approved ICS/wagering rules and should be sourced from the operator's own approved ICS document, not the statute.
- **Patron dispute clock:** if the operator operates or supports the NE racetrack sportsbook's dispute workflow, confirm 48-hour unresolved-dispute-to-Commission-notice and 14-calendar-day investigate-and-respond timers are tracked (§ 11.001.01/.06).
