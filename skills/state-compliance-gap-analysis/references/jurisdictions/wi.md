# Wisconsin — Tribal Gaming (IGRA Compacts) / WI Dept. of Administration, Division of Gaming, Office of Indian Gaming and Regulatory Compliance (OIGRC)

**Code:** WI · **Products:** sportsbook (tribal-only; no commercial/statewide-licensed operator regime, no legal real-money online casino) · **Key authority:** Wis. Stat. §945.01(1)(h) (created by **2025 Wisconsin Act 247**, signed 4/9/2026); Indian Gaming Regulatory Act, 25 U.S.C. §2701 et seq.; individual Tribal-State Class III gaming compacts for WI's 11 federally recognized tribes (compact-by-compact, not a single statewide statute)

## Payments-relevant requirements

**Threshold status (confirm before reading further):** Wisconsin has **no state statute or agency authorizing a statewide commercial or licensed-operator sports-betting market**. Sports wagering is legal only (a) in person at a tribal casino under that tribe's Class III compact (since 2021 compact amendments), or (b) via a geofenced mobile app while physically on that tribe's Indian land (e.g., Oneida's on-premises app). **2025 Wisconsin Act 247** (signed by Gov. Evers 4/9/2026, creating Wis. Stat. §945.01(1)(h)) opens a path to **statewide mobile** wagering via a "hub-and-spoke" model — a bet by a person physically anywhere in Wisconsin is not an illegal "bet" if the wagering server sits on qualifying tribal land and the wager is conducted under a compact originally entered into before 4/1/1993. **As of August 2026 this is not yet operational anywhere**: each of the 11 tribes must separately negotiate and sign a compact amendment, obtain NIGC/Secretary of the Interior review (45–90 days) and Federal Register publication, before that tribe's statewide-mobile product can go live (HIGH — WisBar, InfoLawGroup, TribalBusinessNews, Covers reporting on the bill and post-signing process). Analysts project late-2026/2027 at the earliest for any tribe's launch. Additionally, a constitutional challenge to Act 247 (Wisconsin Institute for Law & Liberty, on behalf of Brown County Taxpayers Association and Citizens Defending Liberty, filed Waukesha County Circuit Court, August 2026) argues the 1993 WI constitutional amendment restricting the legislature's gambling-authorization power requires voter approval for this expansion, plus a 14th Amendment Equal Protection claim; **pending, unresolved as of 8/26/2026** (HIGH confidence the suit exists and is pending; LOW/MEDIUM on outcome/timing).

Because Wisconsin regulates this product almost entirely at the tribe-by-tribe compact/ordinance level rather than through a published statewide rulebook (contrast CO's 1 CCR 207-2 or MD's COMAR 36.10), most of the granular payments requirements below are **not publicly codified at the state level** — they would live inside each tribe's specific compact and internal gaming ordinance, which are not uniformly published. Treat this jurisdiction's requirements as "verify per tribe" until a given tribe's Act 247 compact amendment is signed and public.

### Licensing & change management

- No statewide licensing regime for sports-wagering operators/platforms exists; WI's DOA Division of Gaming (via OIGRC) performs concurrent state-tribal regulation of Class III gaming generally (audits, vendor certification, revenue-share collection) under each compact, not a sports-wagering-specific statute (MEDIUM — DOA/OIGRC mandate; per-product change-management tiers not located).
- Act 247 does not itself create a licensing/certification regime — it only redefines "bet"; platform certification, if any, is whatever the eventual tribe-specific compact amendment specifies (LOW, verify per tribe once compacts are signed and published).
- Any change to a live tribal sportsbook platform is governed by that tribe's compact/gaming ordinance and NIGC oversight (25 U.S.C. §2710), not a WI state statute (MEDIUM).

### Deposits & permitted payment methods

- No WI statute or regulation specifies permitted deposit methods for sports wagering (unlike CO Rule 7.6 or MD COMAR 36.10.18.05) — Act 247 only redefines "bet"; payment-method rules, if any, are internal to each tribe's compact/gaming ordinance and are not centrally published (LOW, verify per tribe).
- No known WI law addresses credit-card deposit bans or deposit-frequency caps (contrast CO SB 26-131) for this product.
- Tribal casinos meeting the BSA "casino" definition are federally subject to 31 CFR Part 1021 (Bank Secrecy Act rules for casinos), including a written AML program (31 CFR 1010.210) — federal, not WI-specific, but binds every WI tribal operator regardless of the state's thin statutory layer (HIGH federal baseline; MEDIUM whether/how it's applied to sports-wagering deposit rails vs. cage cash only).

### Withdrawals & payout timelines

- No WI statute/regulation sets a payout-timeline SLA for sports-wagering winnings (contrast CO's 24-hour Rule 6.9 demand-payment rule or MD's 7-day COMAR 36.10.18.05(J)). Any withdrawal-timeline commitment is a tribe-level/operator-level internal control, not a WI legal requirement (LOW, verify per tribe).

### Player funds segregation / reserve

- No WI state-law reserve/segregation requirement for sports-wagering liabilities was located (contrast CO Rule 6.9's liability-based reserve or MD COMAR 36.10.14.06's $500k floor). If one exists, it is a tribal-compact/ordinance term, not a published state rule (LOW, verify per tribe).

### Responsible gambling

- WI runs no state agency comparable to CO's LGCC or MD's MLGCA for sports-wagering RG programs, and no statewide deposit/spend/time-limit or self-exclusion mandate for this product was located. Some WI tribal casinos maintain their own voluntary self-exclusion programs for their properties, but there is no single statewide self-exclusion registry covering all 11 tribes' sports wagering (LOW/MEDIUM — verify per tribe; general problem-gambling resources exist via WI Dept. of Health Services but are not sports-wagering-specific regulation).

### KYC / age / geolocation

- 21+ to wager on sports at WI tribal casinos, consistent across existing compacts/operator practice (MEDIUM — secondary reporting; exact compact language not independently verified per tribe).
- **Geolocation is the load-bearing control for this entire jurisdiction.** Under Act 247's §945.01(1)(h) hub-and-spoke test, legality turns on two independent facts that must both be evaluated at wager time: (1) the bettor is physically located within Wisconsin, and (2) the wagering server is physically on qualifying tribal land under a pre-4/1/1993 compact (HIGH — this is the operative statutory text per LegiScan/InfoLawGroup summaries of the bill).
- Until a specific tribe's compact amendment authorizing remote/hub-and-spoke wagering is signed and in effect, that tribe's mobile wagering remains legal **only while the bettor is physically on that tribe's Indian land** (e.g., Oneida's existing geofenced on-premises app) (HIGH).
- A WI resident wagering while physically outside Wisconsin, or any wager routed through a server not on qualifying tribal land, falls outside §945.01(1)(h)'s exception and reverts to WI's general illegal-gambling statute, ch. 945 (MEDIUM, inferred from the statutory text; verify against final signed compact terms).

### AML overlays (state-specific, beyond federal BSA)

- No WI-specific AML overlay beyond federal BSA/31 CFR 1021 was located; WI does not license/regulate sports wagering as a standalone state commercial product, so there is no state-agency AML rule paralleling, e.g., CO Rule 7.5(8) (LOW/MEDIUM — re-verify once any tribe's Act 247 compact amendment is published, as compacts sometimes import BSA-equivalent or stricter language).

### Records, reporting & data

- No WI state statute mandates sports-wagering-specific recordkeeping/reporting comparable to CO's 3-year malfunction-log rule (Rule 7.6(1)(d)) or MD's daily reserve-recalculation logs. Applicable recordkeeping instead sits in the federal BSA/CTR regime (FinCEN Form 112 for >$10k aggregate cash-in/out per gaming day, filed within 15 days; 5-year BSA recordkeeping under 31 CFR 1021.230) and NIGC's Minimum Internal Control Standards (25 CFR Part 542, applicable to Class III gaming generally) — MEDIUM, federal/NIGC rather than WI-state-specific.

## Code review focus

- **Geo/jurisdiction gate is the whole story for WI.** Payment rails default-on everywhere else must be OFF for WI unless the account's detected state = WI AND the specific tribal operator/product has a *signed, in-effect* Act 247 compact amendment authorizing hub-and-spoke remote wagering. Until that per-tribe amendment is confirmed live (none are, as of 8/26/2026), real-money deposits for WI-resident mobile play must be blocked statewide and permitted only for on-tribal-land geofenced flows.
- Do not model WI as a single state-level "legal" boolean once any tribe does go live — legality is compact-by-compact and effective-date-by-effective-date (NIGC/Federal Register publication per tribe). Use a tribe/operator-scoped legal-status flag, and keep a fast kill switch given the pending WILL constitutional challenge that could invalidate Act 247 mid-flight.
- No state-mandated deposit method allowlist, withdrawal SLA, reserve formula, or RG-limit API exists to build against for WI (unlike CO/MD) — do not port CO/MD-style requirements onto WI by default; treat any such control here as a tribe/operator-specific contractual term pending confirmation.
- Illegal-market / federal exposure checklist for any WI-adjacent payment flow: UIGEA (turns on whether the bet is legal "where initiated and received" — the hub-and-spoke server-location fiction is untested at the federal UIGEA level); Wire Act interstate-wire exposure; and general illegal-gambling exposure under Wis. Stat. ch. 945 for any WI-resident deposit/wager flow that fails either prong of §945.01(1)(h) (bettor-in-state / server-on-qualifying-tribal-land).
- Confirm federal BSA/CTR logic (31 CFR 1021, FinCEN Form 112, 15-day filing, $10k aggregate/gaming-day threshold) covers sports-wagering cash-in/out at any WI tribal operator's cage, not just table-game drop, since WI's own statutory layer supplies no equivalent state-level backstop.
