**Code:** UT · **Products:** none — total prohibition, no legal gambling of any kind exists in Utah: no state lottery, no commercial or tribal casino, no pari-mutuel, no sportsbook, no legal DFS, no sweepstakes-casino/dual-currency carve-out (HIGH). · **Key authority:** Utah Const. ## Status precisely stated

Utah has banned gambling in its constitution since statehood (1896) and has never authorized any exception. Article VI, § 27 is directed at the Legislature itself — it cannot authorize "any game of chance, lottery or gift enterprise under any pretense or for any purpose" — so legalizing anything requires a constitutional amendment (two-thirds vote in both chambers, then a statewide majority vote), not an ordinary bill. Confidence: HIGH (text confirmed via the Utah Legislature's own section title "[Games of chance not authorized.]" and corroborating secondary sources; direct primary-source page fetch was blocked to navigation-only content, so the verbatim text is HIGH-but-secondary-corroborated rather than directly screen-scraped from le.utah.gov).

Utah and Hawaii are the only two states with no legal gambling of any kind — no lottery (including multi-state draws like Powerball), no commercial or tribal casino, no racetrack/pari-mutuel, no sportsbook, no legal DFS, and no sweepstakes-casino carve-out. Confidence: HIGH.

**Tribal gaming:** unlike most other no-market states, Utah has no operating IGRA Class III tribal casino inside its borders. The Ute Indian Tribe of the Uintah and Ouray Reservation (based in Utah) has pursued casino proposals across the state line in Dinosaur, Colorado specifically because Utah's own prohibition forecloses an in-state option. Confidence: MEDIUM — corroborated by multiple news accounts (Deseret News, GGB Magazine, Indianz) but not independently checked against a primary BIA compact registry confirming zero Utah compacts exist; treat "no Utah tribal gaming" as well-supported but verify with compliance/legal if a tribal-gaming question ever becomes live.

**Statutory layer:** Utah Code criminalizes gambling directly, and — distinctively — treats *providing* online gambling access more harshly than *participating* in it. Per the (now-recodified) text: participating in "gambling or fringe gambling, including any Internet or online gambling" is a class B misdemeanor (class A on a second-or-later conviction), while a person who "intentionally provides or offers to provide any form of Internet or online gambling to any person in this state" commits a **third-degree felony**, and the statute expressly states it applies "regardless of whether a federal law is enacted that authorizes Internet gambling in the states" (Utah's own opt-out language). Confidence: HIGH on the substance (quoted from FindLaw's rendering of former § 76-10-1102); MEDIUM on the exact new section number this maps to under the 2025 recodification (secondary sources point to the new Part 14, e.g. §§ 76-9-1401 "Definitions" and 76-9-1404 "Online gambling promotion" — **verify the precise current section number with compliance/legal before citing it in a comment or ticket**).

**2026 legislative activity confirms tightening, not loosening.** HB 243 (sponsored by Rep. Joseph Elison) passed the House 63-9 (Feb 10, 2026), passed the Senate, and was signed by Gov. Cox, taking effect May 6, 2026; it defines a "proposition bet" (a wager on an individual action/statistic/occurrence not tied directly to a game's outcome) and folds it into the gambling ban — targeting both pick'em-style DFS apps and CFTC-regulated event-contract/prediction-market platforms (Kalshi, Polymarket, and sportsbook-operated prediction markets). Kalshi sued Utah in federal court (filed ~Feb 2026) arguing federal preemption of CFTC-regulated products; that litigation was still pending as of this research and could narrow HB 243's reach for federally-regulated event contracts specifically — it does **not** put ordinary sportsbook/casino/DFS products in doubt. Confidence: HIGH that HB 243 passed/took effect; LOW/unresolved on the litigation's outcome — re-verify before relying on HB 243 against any CFTC-regulated product.

**Sweepstakes-casino / dual-currency:** secondary reporting is inconsistent here and primary sources were unreachable. A Gambling Insider headline ("Oklahoma and Utah Escalate Crackdown on Sweepstakes Casinos in 2026") and a Lexology summary both returned HTTP 403 on fetch and were **not used as sourced content** — treat any specific bill number or enactment date attributed to them as unverified. Other secondary sources describe a distinct "Gambling Revisions" bill that passed the House 63-9 but stalled in the Senate Rules Committee (not enacted), plus reports that the Utah AG has issued subpoenas to sweepstakes-casino operators and that class-action suits are pending in Utah federal court arguing dual-currency models already violate the existing gambling statute. Separately, and higher-confidence: Utah's gambling statute already contains a broad, pre-2026 "fringe gambling" concept (referenced directly in the felony/misdemeanor text above) that appears capable of reaching dual-currency mechanics without needing new legislation. Confidence: MEDIUM that dual-currency sweepstakes products are already illegal under existing "fringe gambling" language; LOW on any specific new 2026 sweepstakes-only statute, bill number, or enactment date — mark "verify" rather than citing one.

**No legalization push exists or is expected.** Multiple secondary sources note no state lawmaker has introduced sports-betting legalization since PASPA was struck down in 2018, and the constitutional-amendment bar plus the LDS Church's consistent doctrinal opposition to gambling (Utah is majority-LDS) make near-term legalization of any product very unlikely. Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH on the political/cultural framing (multiple concordant secondary sources); this is context, not a citable legal fact.

## Code review focus

- **Utah needs the single hardest DEFAULT-DENY geo/jurisdiction block in the entire jurisdiction set.** This is a TOTAL-PROHIBITION state with NO tribal carve-out, NO DFS carve-out, and NO sweepstakes carve-out — there is no product-type nuance to preserve. Sportsbook, online/retail casino, DFS (including pick'em), and sweepstakes-style/dual-currency products must ALL be blocked for any Utah-located session, full stop.
- Treat a Utah geo-gate failure as categorically worse than a typical "unlicensed state" gap: because *providing* online gambling access to a Utah-located person is itself charged as a third-degree felony for the provider under Utah's gambling statute, any code path that would let a deposit or wager/contest-entry succeed for a UT-geolocated session is a live felony-exposure defect — escalate immediately, not a backlog item (illegal-market / UIGEA / federal Wire Act exposure framing applies here too, on top of the state felony).
- Verify the geolocation service's jurisdiction table has an explicit `UT` entry mapped to "no product enabled" for every product flag (sportsbook, casino, DFS/pick'em, sweepstakes/dual-currency) — not merely "absent from allow-list," which risks fail-open behavior.
- Confirm deposit-authorization and wager/contest-entry code paths both independently consult the geo/jurisdiction gate — a gap where only one of the two checks UT would let a deposit succeed while entry fails (or vice versa), producing stranded funds or a misleading "deposit accepted" state in a total-prohibition jurisdiction.
- If the operator has (or ever adds) any DFS/pick'em-style product, do not rely on Utah's historical DFS "gray area" — HB 243 (eff. 5/6/2026) folded proposition-bet/pick'em contests into the gambling ban; the UT block must reflect the post-HB-243 posture, not the pre-2026 assumption.

Flag to legal before ever enabling such a product for UT, regardless of what any other state's rules say.
- If any legacy/test config or feature flag enables UT for any product (e.g., leftover from a states list templated off "all states minus explicit blocklist"), escalate immediately — this is the single jurisdiction in the set with zero legal basis for any exception.
- Watch the pending Kalshi v.
