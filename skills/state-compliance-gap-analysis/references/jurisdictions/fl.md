# Florida — Florida Gaming Control Commission (FGCC) / Seminole Tribe of Florida (Hard Rock Bet, sole authorized operator)

**Code:** FL · **Products:** sportsbook · **Key authority:** 2021 Gaming Compact (Seminole Tribe of Florida–State of Florida, ratified via Fla. Stat. §285.710 / SB 2A); Fla. Stat. §16.712 (FGCC duties); federal IGRA (25 U.S.C. §2701 et seq.) + Wire Act (18 U.S.C. §1084); Seminole Tribal Gaming Code & STGC rules (not public); NIGC Class III Minimum Internal Control Standards

## Payments-relevant requirements

### Licensing & change management

- No conventional state commercial sportsbook license regime. The Seminole Tribe is the sole authorized operator of statewide mobile and retail sports betting under the 30-year 2021 Compact (effective through 7/31/2051) (HIGH — Compact Summary, FL Senate).
- Regulatory chain differs from commercial-license states: the Seminole Tribal Gaming Commission (STGC, a tribal agency) promulgates the operative rules/internal controls for sports betting operations; NIGC approves the Seminole Tribal Gaming Code and issues Class III Minimum Internal Control Standards; the FGCC is designated the "State Compliance Agency" that REVIEWS STGC rules and PROPOSES (does not unilaterally impose) additional consumer protections (Fla. Stat. §285.710(7); §16.712) (HIGH).
- Covered Gaming Activity (incl. sports betting) must comply with the federal Wire Act, 18 U.S.C. §1084 (Compact Summary) (HIGH).
- "Hub-and-spoke" model: mobile wagers are legally deemed placed where the servers reside — on Seminole tribal land — regardless of the bettor's physical location elsewhere within FL. This legal fiction is what lets IGRA (a tribal-lands gaming statute) authorize a statewide mobile product, and it survived the West Flagler litigation (D.C. Cir. reversed the 2021 district court ruling in June 2023; Seminole/West Flagler settled Oct. 2024; SCOTUS declined further review) (HIGH — Compact Summary; multiple litigation trackers).
- Compliance audits are required for slot machine and sports betting operations, feeding the STGC/FGCC oversight chain; the exact change-management/re-certification tiering for platform or app updates is NOT published — STGC's internal-control rules are not public documents (confidence LOW, verify directly with STGC/NIGC if a specific gate is needed).
- The Tribe pays FGCC an annual oversight assessment (up to $600,000, +$150,000 per added facility) that funds the State Compliance Agency's operations (Compact Summary) (HIGH).

### Deposits & permitted payment methods

- No published FL statute or FGCC rule enumerates permitted or prohibited deposit instruments for sports betting. Funding rules are set by Hard Rock Bet/Seminole Gaming as operator policy under STGC's non-public internal controls, not state law (confidence MEDIUM on the absence — confirmed against the primary compact/statute text reviewed, but STGC's non-public rules could contain something not visible here).
- Observed current operator practice (NOT law — confidence LOW/operator-policy, verify against live Hard Rock Bet T&Cs before treating as a requirement): debit cards (Visa/Mastercard/Amex/Discover debit), ACH bank transfer, PayPal, Venmo (bank-funded only — card-funded PayPal/Venmo is blocked), Apple Pay, and Play+ prepaid card. Hard Rock Bet discontinued credit-card deposits as a company-wide policy, not a FL-specific legal mandate.
- Unlike CO (SB 26-131) or other commercial-license states, there is no FL statute/regulation that bans or gates credit-card funding — there is no commercial licensing statute governing sportsbook payment instruments in the first place for this operator.

### Withdrawals & payout timelines

- No FL statute or regulation sets a payout SLA analogous to MD's 7-day rule or CO's 24-hour demand rule. Withdrawal timing is operator practice, not compact- or statute-mandated (confidence MEDIUM/LOW).
- Observed operator practice (Hard Rock Bet, NOT law — confidence LOW, verify against live T&Cs): ACH ≈1 hour–5 business days; PayPal/Venmo/Play+ ≈1 hour–48 hours; some instant payouts via a Nuvei integration added in 2022; withdrawals generally must route back to the funding method used for the deposit.

### Player funds segregation / reserve

- No published state-law reserve or segregation-of-funds requirement was located (contrast CO Rule 6.9, MD COMAR 36.10.14.06). The Compact Summary and Fla. Stat. §285.710/§16.712 do not address player-liability solvency; if any such requirement exists it would live in STGC's non-public internal controls and/or NIGC Class III MICS bankroll provisions (confidence LOW, verify directly with STGC/NIGC).
- IGRA's tribal-sovereignty structure means there is no state insurance/bonding regime analogous to commercial-license states. A payment-processor reserve computed for state audit purposes (as required in CO/MD) has no confirmed FL statutory counterpart.

### Responsible gambling

- The FGCC maintains/oversees a statewide voluntary self-exclusion program, and Hard Rock Bet must refuse wagers from patrons on a FL exclusion or self-exclusion list (HIGH — Fla. Stat. §285.710 compliance framework, corroborated by operator-facing guidance).
- The Tribe must make an annual donation to the Florida Council on Compulsive Gambling (as assignee of the State) of $250,000 per operational gaming facility — an indirect, assignee-funded RG mechanism rather than a direct state RG fund (HIGH — Compact Summary); 24/7 helpline 1-888-ADMIT-IT (888-236-4848) is the standard referral.
- FGCC reviews STGC's problem-gambling procedures and PROPOSES additional consumer protections (advertising/marketing limits, underage-prevention procedures) to STGC, but has no unilateral power to impose them on the Tribe — proposal, not command, authority (Fla. Stat. §285.710(7)) (HIGH).
- Hard Rock Bet offers in-app deposit limits, time-outs, and account-level self-exclusion as an operator feature; these are not separately codified in the FL statutory text reviewed (confidence MEDIUM).
- No FL-specific cooling-off or reverse-withdrawal statute was located (confidence LOW).

### KYC / age / geolocation

- 21+ required to wager on Covered Games, including sports betting, "unless otherwise permitted by state law" (Compact Summary; Fla. Stat. §285.710(13)(b)(7)) (HIGH).
- Geolocation is required to confirm the bettor is physically located within FL before a wager is accepted. Because of the hub-and-spoke legal fiction, geolocation/routing must ALSO functionally attribute the wager to tribal-land servers for the IGRA analysis to hold — a dual purpose (state-boundary geofence + IGRA situs) not present in commercial-license states' geolocation rules (HIGH on the geofencing requirement itself; MEDIUM on the dual-purpose characterization, which is an inference from the Compact Summary and litigation record rather than a verbatim compact clause).
- FGCC reviews/proposes underage-prevention procedures (Fla. Stat. §285.710(7)), but the specific ID-verification method/standard is set by STGC's non-public rules — confidence LOW on the exact KYC method requirements (document types, database checks, etc.).

### AML overlays

- No FL-specific state AML statute for tribal sports betting was found in the primary sources reviewed (Fla. Stat. §285.710, §16.712, Compact Summary). The operative AML regime is FEDERAL, not state: tribal casinos/sportsbooks with gross annual gaming revenue over $1M are "financial institutions" under the Bank Secrecy Act (31 CFR §1010.100(t)(5)-(6); 31 CFR Part 1021), requiring a written AML program, CTRs (aggregated >$10k cash-in/out per gaming day), and SARs, administered by FinCEN — NOT by the FGCC (HIGH on BSA applicability to tribal casinos generally; MEDIUM on how cleanly the cage/slot-cash-oriented CTR/SAR mechanics map onto a mobile-only sportsbook with minimal cash handling — verify with compliance/legal counsel).
- The FGCC's "integrity monitoring" duty (evaluating abnormal betting patterns, notifying law enforcement) is a match-fixing/integrity control, not a laundering-specific AML overlay, though it functionally overlaps with suspicious-activity detection (Fla. Stat. §16.712) (MEDIUM).

### Records, reporting & data

- FGCC must submit an annual report (due Dec. 1) to state leadership covering gaming industry developments, Commission actions, state revenues/expenses by gaming type, licensee performance, disciplinary actions, and investigation summaries (Fla. Stat. §16.712) (HIGH).
- The Tribe must have compliance audits prepared for slot machine and sports betting operations (Compact Summary) (HIGH); audit scope, frequency, and recipient are not detailed in the public sources reviewed (confidence LOW).
- FGCC holds subpoena power to compel production of books, papers, and records relevant to its oversight duties (Fla. Stat. §16.712) (HIGH).
- No FL-specific data-retention period (e.g., CO's 3-year malfunction-log rule) was located in the public sources reviewed (confidence LOW).

## Code review focus

- Do not model FL as a commercial-license state: no code path should assume a state-issued sportsbook license number, a state-set deposit-instrument allow/deny list, or a state-set withdrawal SLA for FL — those regulatory knobs don't exist in FL's compact-based model. Any generic "per-state withdrawal SLA" or "payout deadline" table should mark FL as N/A rather than defaulting to 0/immediate or silently inheriting another state's value.
- Self-exclusion gate: block wager acceptance for patrons on FL's statewide voluntary exclusion list, same enforcement point as other states — but confirm which feed the code actually integrates against (FGCC list vs. Florida Council on Compulsive Gambling list vs. Hard Rock Bet's own internal list); the sources reviewed do not confirm these are one unified feed.
- Age gate: enforce 21+ specifically for FL sports betting (not 18, used elsewhere in FL for non-gaming contexts) at both KYC and wager-acceptance checkpoints.
- Geolocation gate must (a) block wagers physically located outside FL, and (b) if the codebase logs a wager's "location" for compliance/audit purposes, confirm it also captures server/routing location — not just the bettor's coordinates — since the dual record (bettor-in-state + server-on-tribal-land) is central to the compact's legal theory.
- BSA/AML: if <payments-lib>'s CTR/SAR/AML logic is gated on state-license metadata, confirm FL's federal-BSA-only status (no state AML statute) doesn't cause the code to skip BSA checks it would otherwise run for a "licensed" state. FL should still hit federal CTR/SAR thresholds despite lacking a state gaming AML statute.
- Do not port CO/MD-style reserve-calculation or payout-SLA logic to FL without an explicit override — no confirmed statutory analog exists. If such logic runs universally across states, FL needs a documented exception/TODO rather than a silent pass-through.
- Flag any hardcoded assumption that "has a sports betting license" implies a state-regulator relationship; for FL, the operator identity itself (Seminole Tribe / Hard Rock Bet) is the compliance unit, not a separately licensed brand — relevant wherever the codebase keys authorization/compliance rules off a "license number" or "operator license status" field.
