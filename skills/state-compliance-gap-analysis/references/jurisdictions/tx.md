# Texas — No authorized statewide sports betting or commercial casino gaming; Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR, state lottery/charitable bingo, since the Texas Lottery Commission's 2025 abolition) / Texas Racing Commission (pari-mutuel)

**Code:** TX · **Products:** none — no legal statewide or online real-money sports betting or casino gaming exists in Texas as of 2026 (HIGH). The only lawful broad-based real-money gaming channels are the state lottery/charitable bingo (now under TDLR) and pari-mutuel horse/greyhound racing (Texas Racing Commission). ## Status precisely stated

Texas has **no legal statewide or online real-money sports betting and no legal commercial or tribal casino gaming in 2026**. The prohibition is constitutional, not just statutory: Tex. Const. art. III, §47 requires the Legislature to prohibit "lotteries and gift enterprises" except for the narrow carve-outs it separately authorizes (state lottery, charitable bingo, charitable raffles); anything not affirmatively carved out is presumptively illegal gambling under Penal Code Ch. 47 (Justia; Texas State Law Library gambling guide). Confidence: HIGH.

Because the prohibition sits in the constitution, ordinary legislation cannot legalize sports betting or casino gaming — it requires a joint resolution passing both chambers by a two-thirds supermajority, then a statewide voter referendum (Texas State Law Library; multiple 2026 legislative trackers). The 2026 session saw renewed pushes — HB 1942 (Rep. Jeff Leach) and SB 888 (Sen. Carol Alvarado) proposed a regulated mobile sports-betting framework (up to 15 operator licenses, $10M application fee, 10% GGR tax) — but Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick controls the Senate calendar and has repeatedly blocked gambling bills from reaching a vote; industry trackers put realistic legalization at 2029 at the earliest, contingent on a change in Senate leadership (Bright Side of News; Sports Betting Dime; Bookmakers Review, all Aug 2026). Confidence: HIGH on the constitutional-amendment mechanism and current bills' existence; MEDIUM on the 2029 timeline estimate, which is industry commentary, not a legislative fact.

Pari-mutuel wagering on horse/greyhound racing at licensed tracks is a distinct, narrower carve-out under the Texas Racing Act, regulated by the Texas Racing Commission (txrc.texas.gov) — separately from sports betting/casino, and NOT a product the operator offers. The Texas Lottery itself was restructured in 2025: the Legislature declined to pass a Sunset bill for the standalone Texas Lottery Commission, effectively abolishing it, and transferred lottery + charitable bingo administration to the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) via SB 3070, effective 9/1/2025, alongside new restrictions on third-party lottery-courier services (Texas Tribune, 6/25/2026 and 5/25/2025 reporting; Hoodline). Confidence: HIGH on the TDLR transfer and effective date; LOW on the precise scope of the new courier restrictions — verify against SB 3070's enrolled text before relying on it.

DFS occupies a genuinely disputed middle ground, not a settled illegal or legal status: Texas AG Ken Paxton's Opinion KP-0057 (1/19/2016) concluded a Texas court would likely find paid DFS contests (DraftKings, FanDuel) constitute illegal gambling under Penal Code §47.02, because the statute's "bet" definition turns on any element of chance and DFS outcomes are affected by injuries, weather, and officiating (texasattorneygeneral.gov; National Law Review). An AG opinion is advisory, not binding law, and major DFS operators have continued operating in Texas essentially uninterrupted since 2016 with no statewide enforcement shutdown (multiple sources). Treat DFS in Texas as **live legal risk, not settled legality** — competitor presence is not a legal defense. Confidence: HIGH on the opinion's existence/holding and on continued operator presence; MEDIUM on why no enforcement has followed (no primary source explains the gap; plausibly prosecutorial discretion/DFS industry self-regulation via a state trade-association model, not confirmed here).

## Code review focus

- **Default-on rails are the risk, not a gap in TX-specific rules.** If the payments platform's gateway/product config enables deposit, wager, and payout rails by default across states and disables per-jurisdiction, Texas needs the reverse posture: sportsbook and online-casino products must be geo/jurisdiction-blocked by default, with no code path that silently activates them for a TX-resident/TX-located session.
- Verify the geolocation/jurisdiction table has an explicit `TX` entry mapped to "no product enabled" for sportsbook and online-casino product flags — not merely "absent from allow-list," which risks fail-open behavior.
- Confirm deposit authorization and wager-placement code paths both independently consult the geo/jurisdiction gate for TX — a gap where only one of the two checks TX would let a deposit succeed while wager placement fails (or vice versa), producing stranded funds.
- Any legacy/test config or feature flag that enables TX for sportsbook or online casino (e.g., a states list templated as "all states minus explicit blocklist") is a live illegal-market / UIGEA (18 U.S.C. §1955 state-law-predicate) / federal Wire Act exposure item — escalate immediately if found, same severity framing as any other constitutionally-prohibited state.
- If the operator ever offers a DFS-adjacent product, do not default TX to "open" on the strength of competitor presence — the 2016 AG opinion (KP-0057) means TX is a live legal-risk state for DFS specifically, distinct from (and less settled than) the flat sportsbook/casino prohibition; this should be its own product-level flag, reviewed with legal before any DFS rail is enabled for TX.
- Do not reuse a TX sportsbook/casino block flag as a proxy for pari-mutuel/ADW or lottery-courier legality if the operator ever touches either — those sit under separate authorities (Texas Racing Commission; TDLR post-SB 3070) with their own rules, including new 2025 lottery-courier restrictions not yet detailed here.
- Watch the constitutional-amendment path (two-thirds vote both chambers + statewide referendum) for any 2027+ legislative session; HB 1942/SB 888-style bills are a recurring pattern, not a one-time event, and this file, the geo-jurisdiction table, and the code-review items above would all need revisiting before any TX sportsbook/casino flag is flipped on.
